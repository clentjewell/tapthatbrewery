## Why a content system, not a content calendar

Tap That Brewery’s buying cycle is the design constraint: ~60% of buyers take up to three months to commit, 40% longer. Today that consideration window is "nurtured by memory" (D05) – the in-venue pathway (taste → demo → giveaway → 50% close) has no digital equivalent. Content is that equivalent. It must serve social (#41), the website, and email/SMS lifecycle from one production effort, because the whole machine runs on Raef at ~70% capacity plus defined Jewell support.

Three content jobs, one seasonal overlay:

## 1. Hero content – the stuff people share

| Asset | What it is | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| **Giveaway cycles** | The proven demand event: ~1,000 entries, 50%-off close, 22–25 systems per cycle. Each cycle is a content season: launch film, countdown, winner install documentary | 2×/yr (#43, #45) |
| **The award story** | Midnight in Tokyo – Brewer’s Choice at Crafted in year one, told through Chris (ex Burleigh Barrels) on the brewery floor. The credibility spine under every taste claim | Evergreen; refresh each awards season |
| **Build showcases** | Real customers' installs: the tradie shed reveal, the family deck, the integrated kitchen system, the Benchy-equipped 4WD rig. Esky-to-envy, filmed at theirs | 1/month (the monthly shoot’s centrepiece) |

## 2. Help content – the stuff people search

Owned on the website (SEO + nurture links), cut down for social, linked from lifecycle messages. Jewell drafts, client verifies technically, Raef fronts where a face is needed.

| Asset | Answers | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Setup guides** | "How do I install a 2-tap system?" | One per system format; doubles as post-purchase onboarding email content; hosted as unlisted video embeds |
| **Keg care** | Line cleaning, plastic vs corny (drop COB Tue → ready Fri, $3 clean), gas, pouring temperature | Reduces support load; keeps quality experience consistent with the award positioning |
| **"Which system?" chooser** | 2-tap $975 → 6-tap $2,550, mini 5L $225, integrated from $1,750, Benchy | The space objection answered by format range; assembly & delivery $300; Afterpay/Zip noted |
| **Tasting notes & what’s pouring** | "What do I refill with?" | Digitise the in-venue QR notes + the Kegged & Ready board into an always-current range page – directly attacks the "same-again fatigue" churn pain (#06) |

## 3. Objection content – the stuff that closes

Built for retargeting and the nurture sequence, matched to the four purchase-blocking objections (#06):

| Objection | Content answer |
|---|---|
| "A grand up front" | **The cost calculator**: interactive per-litre maths – 20L keg = 44 schooners; ~$275 equivalent vs ~$500 for a 50L Balter through a wholesaler; no excise, no wholesaler margin. Plus Afterpay/Zip and (when GoTab enables it) lease-to-buy. Cost content closes – it never opens (Christy’s rule) |
| "My wife won’t let me" | **The partner-pleaser range**: 27 taps, ~1/3 non-beer – seltzers, Strawberry Gin Fizz, ginger beer, cocktails on tap. Her drinks are on tap too. Health angle (gluten-reduced, lower-carb) held until compliance wording is locked |
| "Nowhere to put it" | **Space & fit guide**: footprint photos by format, bench/integrated/mini options, rental-friendly setups, real installs from the build showcases |
| "I’ll drink too much" | **The census story**: 57% of owners who pour daily or most days pour only 1–2 kegs a month. "Fresher, better – not more." Responsible-consumption framing throughout (#41 rules) |

## Repurposing flows – one shoot, many assets

The monthly batch shoot (~2–3 hrs, venue + one customer location) is the entire production engine. Nothing is shot for a single use.

**Flow A – build showcase:** customer install shoot → 1 Reel (kudos pillar) → 3 stills (feed + stories) → testimonial pull-quote (website + Google reviews prompt) → ad creative candidate → space/fit guide example → UGC seed (customer reposts, earns Tap Tokens).

**Flow B – brewery floor:** 30 min with Chris + the Kegged & Ready board → 1 Reel → tasting-notes page update → "what’s pouring" email block → wholesale one-pager imagery (#44).

**Flow C – giveaway cycle:** launch film → countdown story frames → entrant prompts → winner install (= next month’s Flow A) → 50%-close creative → case-study numbers for the Gate 2 story.

**Flow D – help article:** one written guide → carousel summary → lifecycle email link → FAQ schema on the website (#43 local SEO).

## The monthly production rhythm (capacity-honest)

| Week | Raef (~6–8 hrs/wk within the 70%) | Jewell |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Batch shoot (2–3 hrs); write + Buffer-schedule the month’s 12–13 posts | Shot list + caption skeletons supplied in advance |
| 2 | Stories live; community management; refill-counter UGC asks | Draft the month’s help article + email |
| 3 | Publish help content; monthly email send; Google Business post | Landing page / calculator / automation build per #43 roadmap |
| 4 | Review: what ran, what converted (giveaway entries, demo bookings, refill mentions); log wholesale DM leads | Reporting pull + next month’s shot list |

Giveaway months add the cycle assets on top – Jewell absorbs production overflow so Raef’s ceiling holds. Anything demanding a second operator full-time is Stage 3 of the business plan (#23), not this rhythm.
