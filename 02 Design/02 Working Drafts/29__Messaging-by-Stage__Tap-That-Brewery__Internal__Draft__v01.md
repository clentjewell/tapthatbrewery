## Purpose

The words for each stage of #28's journey, governed by one rule from discovery (Christy, adopted in D06): **cost is the customer's dirty little secret — kudos and connection do the buying, savings justify it afterwards.** Cost closes; it never opens — except for high-volume drinkers, the one profile where the maths leads openly. Voice throughout is the one already on Tap That's walls: cheeky, pun-forward, warm ("Tap That Knockoffs", "Skittle Me This", "5 ways to support us — total $0.00"). Sample lines are drafts in that voice, for testing, not final copy.

## Awareness — hooks (kudos/connection only; no prices)

| Audience | Hook line (draft) | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Young families | *"Your place just became the place."* / *"No babysitter. No closing time. No warm slabs."* | Connection pain, zero cost talk |
| Tradies | *"Finish the shed properly."* / *"The esky was a phase."* | Kudos; the build's crown |
| Boomers | *"Pour them something you're proud of."* | Generous hosting, award-backed quality |
| High-volume drinkers (exception) | *"44 schooners. One keg. Do the maths."* | The one lane where cost opens |
| Partner-inclusive beat (all) | *"A third of our 27 taps aren't even beer. Her round's sorted."* | Pre-empts the veto before it forms |
| Benchy/outdoor | *"Beer on tap, wherever the ute stops."* | Category of one; no competitor claim needed |

Proof garnish available at this stage: *"Award-winning first year out — Midnight in Tokyo, Brewer's Choice at Crafted."* Use as a trust line, never the headline.

## Consideration — one message per objection

The 3-month window is objection-handling territory. Each of the four discovery objections gets exactly one message, sequenced in #30.

| Objection | Message (draft) | Proof behind it |
|---|---|---|
| Upfront cost (~$975) | *"We sell the system at cost. Not a typo — we make our money when you love the beer."* Follow: Afterpay/Zip; lease-to-buy when live | Systems at supplier price, photo-verified card; radical-honesty framing converts the objection into trust |
| "My wife won't let me" | *"Bring her in. Nine of our taps are hers — seltzers, gin fizz, ranch water, mojitos on tap."* | ~1/3 of 27 taps non-beer, engineered for exactly this; gluten-reduced/lower-carb held in reserve |
| Nowhere to put it | *"If a bar fridge fits, a 2-tap fits. Benchy fits in the tray."* + install-photo UGC | 2-tap footprint; integrated options from $1,750; real installs as evidence |
| "I'll drink too much" | *"We asked 50 keg owners. 70% pour daily — and still only go through 1–2 kegs a month. It's a tap, not a dare."* | Census (n=50) moderation data — Tap That's single most disarming proof point |

## Purchase moment — the close

- **Giveaway close (proven):** *"Didn't win? Everyone taps something — 50% off your system, this week only."* (Converted 22–25 systems last round; keep the mechanic, tighten the deadline.)
- **Payment softeners:** *"Take it home today, square it up in bits — Afterpay and Zip at the bar."* Lease-to-buy line held until GoTab confirms terms.
- **Attach at the till (say it every time):** *"Keg Crew from day one — $30 off every refill, your birthday keg's on us. It pays for itself by autumn."* (At 2 refills/month, $250/yr repays in ~4 months — the maths is true, so say it plainly.)
- **Bonus credit reframe:** *"Your $40 credit's waiting on your first refill. Don't leave it in the tank."*

## Habit — membership and cadence framing

- Frame refills as the ritual, not the transaction: *"Fresh one for the weekend?"* beats "reorder now."
- Membership framing is club, not discount: *"Keg Crew isn't a loyalty card. It's a leaderboard, a birthday keg, and $30 off every fill because you're one of ours."*
- Leaderboard nudges in the house voice: *"Jack P.'s on 7,569 tokens. Are you going to take that?"*
- Range rotation keeps 27 taps working: *"New on tap: Honey Bear IPA. Your kegerator called — it's bored."*

## At-risk (day 60–75, before the window closes)

Tone: warm check-in, never guilt, never "we miss you" desperation. The census says lapsed usually means moderating or busy — respect it.

- *"Been a while between pours. Taking it easy, or just flat out? Either way — the Czech Pilsner's tapping fresh this week."*
- Moderation-friendly branch: *"Going lighter? Smart. Ginger beer and seltzers refill the same as beer — the taps don't judge."*
- Practical branch: *"Drop the corny by Tuesday, it's ready Friday. We'll do the heavy lifting."*

## Winback (90+ days)

Escalate value, keep the cheek, cap the ladder so the discount never becomes the relationship:

1. **Nudge (day 90):** straight re-invite + what's new on tap. No offer.
2. **Offer (day ~105):** current mechanic — *"$20 off your next fill, this fortnight only. The taps have been asking about you."*
3. **Partner offer (day ~120):** the Marie's Pizza/Buttercard play — *"Keg + family-size pizza on us. Sunday sorted."* Family-framed winback fits the ~45% young-families base better than a deeper discount.
4. **Last word:** *"No hard feelings — we'll keep your tokens warm."* Then rest the contact; a re-entry via giveaway costs less than a fifth SMS.

## Voice guardrails (all stages)

- Puns are seasoning, not structure — one per message, maximum.
- Never a bulk-drinking frame; the census moderation stat is the brand's conscience as well as its proof.
- Cost appears as maths (per schooner, per litre, vs a night out), never as "cheap" — and never against the keg shop across the road (positioning rule from #10/D03).
- Gluten-*reduced*, never gluten-free.
