## Why this is its own project

The proposal carries two six-month items in the brand workstream: **designing for her**, and **the unit as an object worth owning**. Both are written there as briefs, because that is all the current engagement can honestly promise. Writing a brief is a month of work. Acting on it is a different kind of project, with different people in it, and it should be decided on its own terms rather than absorbed quietly into a marketing retainer.

This document is what those two briefs grow into if you want them to. It is one project, not two, because the two halves answer the same question from different ends.

**The position makes it necessary.** The best night in, on tap. Home entertainment, not beer. That was settled on 24 September, and it is a claim about where Tap That sits, not a slogan to run in a campaign. A keg system that belongs beside the outdoor kitchen and the coffee machine has to look like it belongs there, and the range poured through it has to suit everyone standing around it. Neither is true yet. Marketing can carry a position for a while. It cannot carry one the product contradicts.

## Stream one: the range, designed for her

Not a campaign, and not a women's beer. The work is to make the range something a household says yes to rather than something one person in it tolerates.

| Workstream | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Range architecture** | About 19 brew choices is costing twice: cost of goods on the tail, and choice paralysis at the point of sale. Decide the heroes, decide what the 27 taps are actually for, and decide what the roughly one third that is not beer is allowed to become. |
| **The non-beer third, taken seriously** | Hydration, low and no alcohol, and the RTD range built as a real story rather than a line on a menu. This is the part of the tap wall that decides whether a second person in the household cares. |
| **A product she would choose** | Co-development with a local fitness or wellness brand, sampling, and a named product that is designed for her rather than adapted for her. Wellness collaborations are the cheapest first proof. |
| **Serve and ritual** | Glassware, pour, garnish, what it looks like handed to someone. The product is not the liquid, it is the moment it is handed over. |
| **The point of sale** | Most popular, award winning, what to eat with it. Missing in the room and missing on the site, and it is the cheapest thing on this page. |

**The test:** would she pour it for her friends without being asked to, and would she be pleased to be seen doing it.

## Stream two: the unit as an object worth owning

The hardware. This is industrial design work, and it is the half that decides which aisle the product is sold in.

| Workstream | What it covers |
|---|---|
| **Design direction** | What a Tap That system looks like when it is chosen rather than tolerated. Materials, finish, proportion, and the font as the one piece that shows. Benchmarked against premium bench appliances, not against other kegerators. |
| **The integrated unit, made deliberate** | The plumbed-in systems already selling become a defined product with a name, a spec, a price and a place on the website. |
| **The outdoor kitchen unit** | A keg-and-tap module sized to sit in an outdoor kitchen as a standard inclusion, the way the bar fridge already does. Renders first, demand tested on social, manufacturing conversations only after that. |
| **Retail readiness** | What has to be true for the product to be ranged in outdoor living rather than appliances: presentation, packaging, display, and the story on the shelf. |
| **Make or partner** | Whether Tap That develops hardware, specifies it with a manufacturer, or licences the design. This is a business decision, and it is the one that decides the size of the project. |

**The test:** does someone show it to a visitor without being prompted.

## How it would run

Four stages, each with a stop-or-continue point. Nothing commits to manufacturing before demand has been tested.

| Stage | What happens | Ends with |
|---|---|---|
| **01 Define** | Range decisions, the design direction, and the make-or-partner call. Desk work and workshops, no production. | A signed direction and a decision on stream two's shape. |
| **02 Visualise** | Renders and product visuals for the outdoor kitchen unit and the integrated system. The range concepts laid out. | Assets good enough to test with, and to show a manufacturer or a retailer. |
| **03 Test** | Demand testing on social and with the existing base. The wellness collaboration run as a live product. | Numbers, not opinions. A go or no-go on each stream. |
| **04 Build** | Production design, supplier engagement, packaging, retail presentation and launch. | Product in market. |

Stages 01 and 02 are the ones worth committing to now. Stage 03 is cheap and decides the rest. Stage 04 is a different order of spend and should not be priced until stage 03 has reported.

## What it produces

- A range architecture with the heroes, the tail and the non-beer third decided.
- A product and design direction document for the hardware, with benchmarks and materials.
- Renders and product visuals for the integrated unit and the outdoor kitchen module.
- A demand test, run and reported, with the numbers behind the go or no-go.
- One co-developed product in market as the first proof of the range work.
- A retail readiness pack for the conversation with national retail.
