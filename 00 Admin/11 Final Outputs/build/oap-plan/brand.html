Brand plan – on a page

The position

Your place, the best catch-up on the street. Home entertainment, not beer.

Every household deserves beer on tap is Justin's line and it stays as the ambition. It is not the brand idea. The brand idea is what the product does to an ordinary Saturday: it turns a catch-up into the one people talk about. That puts Tap That in the home entertainment business, beside the outdoor kitchen, the high-end barbecue and the coffee machine, not beside the bar fridge. Same move BMW made from cars to mobility, and it opens the same doors: partners, product and price.

It also settles what Tap That is not. Not a craft brewery competing on taps, not a bottle shop competing on price, and not a venue competing for a Friday night.

Look for what the category does badly, not what it does well. Harvey Norman's outdoor living page leads with kitchens and appliances, and the kegerator is not in the grouping. That is the gap.

Nobody on the Gold Coast owns this category, in market or in search. Christy owns the wording; this is her draft to sign off.

Who the enemy is

Pubs at $12+ a schooner, bottleshops at $80+ a case, and home brew supply stores. Not the duopoly, not A&A, not the keg delivery operators.

Justin's own words: the more people drinking craft beer and not XXXX, the better. Other breweries are not the enemy. Every price conversation should be unit economics: $2.55 a schooner at home against $12+ at the pub, 2.5 cases for the price of one.

Do not get pulled into a keg price war. It is the one fight where winning costs more than losing.

A&A's domain is suspended and they have merged with Burleigh Homebrew. Following a competitor in that state down on price is worse than pointless.

The at-home figure is settled: $2.55 a schooner. Every piece of collateral and the live ad should carry it.

Heroes, not a tail

About 19 brew choices is costing twice: cost of goods on the tail, and choice paralysis at the point of sale. It also means no single beer gets enough marketing to become known.

Cutting the tail is not the point. The point is putting the freed marketing energy behind the heroes that remain. Romance the beer, not the venue.

Add social proof at the point of sale: most popular, award winning, what to eat with it. Missing in the room and missing on the site.

27 taps and roughly a third not beer is core identity, not clutter. Cut brews, not the breadth of drink types.

Design for her

The single largest unaddressed opportunity in the brand.

The range, the marketing and the hardware all read masculine. Covered is not the same as catered for. Christy's argument is the blunt one: if women are not coming, men will not follow. Justin's target segments, sharehouses, DINKs, young families, baby boomers, high-volume drinkers, cashed-up tradies, are households, and designing for her is how those households say yes.

What it means in practice: romance the RTD properly, build the hydration and light range as a real story, make products she is proud to serve, and design a system she is willing to have in eyesight at home.

And the hardware itself. A Zip HydroTap sells at around $10,000 largely because it looks right on a bench. That is the standard.

Where to start: wellness influencer collaborations, and co-developing something with a local fitness brand. Christy's example was a protein beer. The point is a product she would choose, not a product she tolerates.

This is a product and design brief, not a campaign. It will not be solved by a post.

Proof already owned and barely used
  • Integrated units, already selling. Three to four plumbed-in systems with only the font showing, installed in the last couple of months. Not on the website. This is the product that belongs in the outdoor living grouping.
  • Referrals at 18% of sales without a programme worth the name. The proof that the social mechanic works is already in the numbers.
  • Crafted Brewers' Choice award. Won, and almost invisible. It belongs on the site, in the room, on the tap decals and in social.
  • Fits any system, no CO2 with Talos kegs. Two buying objections answered, both currently below the fold.
  • 1,000 giveaway entries and strong reviews. Demand for the hardware is demonstrated, and the reviews are present but unpromoted and invisible to AI search.
  • Named brand ambassadors and UGC providers. Kurt, Troy, Mitch, Aden and Ash, Justin's own list, ready to deploy within three months.
  • Oktoberfest, with the keg system giveaway. A proof moment in the next 30 days.
Identity housekeeping, and what is still open
  • The name on the mark. Documents say Tap That Brewery, the badge says Brewery & Keghouse. Founders' call. Until it is made, never crop the badge.
  • Two live membership posters quote different prices. One has to be withdrawn.
  • Source files. Vector logos and font names are still outstanding, so the colour and type calls in the guidelines stay provisional.
  • 30 days, per Justin, now Christy's. Rewrite key messaging for each target market on the home entertainment position, and get the customer journey mapped.
Voice, and the rule that governs it

Green leads, gold accents, never the reverse. Bold condensed caps for headlines, a plain workhorse sans for reading. No scripts, no serifs.

Two registers. Cheeky for names and moments, at exactly the level the trading name sets. Plain and generous for money, service and responsibility. Never crude beyond the name's own wink, never beer-snob, never discount-shouty. Justin's own register, be the envy of your friends, cashed-up tradies, sets the ceiling for how far the cheek goes.

Photography: the real room, real customers, available light. Not stock, and not anything that could not have been taken at Burleigh Heads.

The event flyers already built follow this. They are the reference for anything new.
