The position

The pub at your place. Fresher, cheaper by the schooner, and yours.

Tap That is not a brewery that also delivers. It is the way a household stops paying pub prices. That framing changes who the competitor is, what the range should be, and who the marketing is talking to.

It also settles what Tap That is not. Not a craft brewery competing on taps, not a bottle shop competing on price, and not a venue competing for a Friday night.

Nobody on the Gold Coast owns this category, in market or in search. That is the opening.

Who the enemy is

Overpriced pubs. Not the duopoly, not A&A, not the keg delivery operators.

Every price conversation should be unit economics against a $10 to $18 pub schooner. Being dearer than another keg supplier is defensible. Losing a sticker fight is not, and A&A cannot be beaten on sticker without giving away the margin this business runs on.

Do not get pulled into a keg price war. It is the one fight where winning costs more than losing.

A&A's domain is suspended and they have merged with Burleigh Homebrew. The competitor that set the price anchor is not in good health, which makes following them down worse than pointless.

The at-home per-schooner figure still needs settling. Use the comparison, not a number, until it is.

Heroes, not a tail

About 19 brew choices is costing twice: cost of goods on the tail, and choice paralysis at the point of sale. It also means no single beer gets enough marketing to become known.

Cutting the tail is not the point. The point is putting the freed marketing energy behind the heroes that remain. Romance the beer, not the venue.

Add social proof at the point of sale: most popular, award winning, what to eat with it. Missing in the room and missing on the site.

27 taps and roughly a third not beer is core identity, not clutter. Cut brews, not the breadth of drink types.

Design for her

The single largest unaddressed opportunity in the brand.

The range, the marketing and the hardware all read masculine. Covered is not the same as catered for. Christy's argument is the blunt one: if women are not coming, men will not follow.

What it means in practice: romance the RTD properly, build the hydration and light range as a real story, make products she is proud to serve, and design a system she is willing to have in eyesight at home.

Where to start: wellness influencer collaborations, and co-developing something with a local fitness brand. Christy's example was a protein beer. The point is a product she would choose, not a product she tolerates.

This is a product and design brief, not a campaign. It will not be solved by a post.

Proof already owned and barely used
  • Crafted Brewers' Choice award. Won, and almost invisible. It belongs on the site, in the room, on the tap decals and in social.
  • Fits any system. A buying objection answered, currently below the fold.
  • No CO2 with Talos kegs. Gas bottle management is a real barrier for new owners and this removes it.
  • 1,000 giveaway entries. Demand for the hardware is demonstrated, not assumed.
  • Strong reviews. Present, unpromoted, and invisible to AI search.
Identity housekeeping, and what is still open
  • The name on the mark. Documents say Tap That Brewery, the badge says Brewery & Keghouse. Founders' call. Until it is made, never crop the badge.
  • Two live membership posters quote different prices. One has to be withdrawn.
  • Source files. Vector logos and font names are still outstanding, so the colour and type calls in the guidelines stay provisional.
  • Category language. Commercial brewer invites mainstream price comparison. The category needs a name of its own.
Voice, and the rule that governs it

Green leads, gold accents, never the reverse. Bold condensed caps for headlines, a plain workhorse sans for reading. No scripts, no serifs.

Two registers. Cheeky for names and moments, at exactly the level the trading name sets. Plain and generous for money, service and responsibility. Never crude beyond the name's own wink, never beer-snob, never discount-shouty.

Photography: the real room, real customers, available light. Not stock, and not anything that could not have been taken at Burleigh Heads.

The event flyers already built follow this. They are the reference for anything new.
