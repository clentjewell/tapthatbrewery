The two highest-return moves

01 · Convert the owners who are already sold.

Justin's priority one, with a 3-month item: a strategy to transition every existing keg system owner onto Tap That refills. Voucher tied to Harvey Norman sales, the Kegland and corny keg databases, and Benchy. Bunnings-installer model: they sell hardware, we are named first call.

02 · Take the friction out of reordering.

Free delivery and consumption-triggered reminders replace the 90-day SMS. Justin's launch items: lease-to-buy systems and a keg delivery service.

03 · Split the lapsed list in two.

Some are cutting back, needing the low and non-alcoholic range; some just forgot, needing one tap to reorder. The split is not established. Justin's 3-month item: a re-engagement system for lapsed customers.

04 · Referrals, JVPs and online drive system sales.

Justin's priority three and one-year marker for most keg sales. 3-month items: referral programme and Bring a Mate live; JVP/referral list built, outreach started; UGC/ambassador content (Kurt, Troy, Mitch, Aden, Ash) ready.

JP holds Harvey Norman franchisee contacts, plus a contact with 20,000-plus installs across Harvey Norman, JB Hi-Fi and Good Guys.

Wholesale as a function

Bars have approached Tap That and it still has not converted. That is not a demand problem, it is the absence of follow-up and sales experience. Justin's priority five, with a 3-month item: outreach to event venues and niched venues.

Stand it up properly: a full-timer or a commissioned agent, targets and KPIs, a pipeline in the CRM rather than in someone's head. The room put it at about twelve touch points before a venue buys beer, and Tap That has the first one and almost none of the eleven after it. The next hire is undecided between a brewer and wholesale plus bar coverage, hybrid either way.

Then go after the shape of it: custom systems for sporting clubs, club-branded kegs, minimum guarantees per season, and moving rigs from footy to cricket so they earn year-round.

And work around the duopoly. Lock-in is not satisfaction, and venues are already forming buying groups to get around it.

One account is worth about ten households. This is where the return is.

Found by people, and by machines

Within six to twelve months a large share of web traffic will expect an answer from AI search rather than a list of links. The site has to be readable by both.

The basics that are missing: a real About page, an FAQ that answers the buying objections, reviews on the site, and the awards page the Crafted win deserves. Justin's tech item, SEM, runs alongside these GEO basics, not instead of them.

Reviews are the cheapest asset here. They exist, they are strong, and they sit on platforms rather than on the site where a model can read them.

Then the category play: nobody owns beer at home on the Gold Coast in search. Owning it is cheaper now than it will ever be again.
