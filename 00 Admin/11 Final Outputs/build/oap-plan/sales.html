The two highest-return moves

01 · Convert the owners who are already sold.

Gift with purchase or a first-keg-free voucher tied to Harvey Norman system sales. Buy the Kegland and corny keg owner databases. Pursue the Benchy partnership. The model is the one kitchen installers use with Bunnings: they sell the hardware, we are the named first call for what goes in it.

02 · Take the friction out of reordering.

Free delivery works and the margin comes back in dollars, not percent. Replace the blanket 90-day SMS with consumption-triggered reminders, because about 60% reorder within three months and 40% take longer, and one clock fails both. Lead on no CO2. Rethink party rental so a minimum keg guarantee covers the hire fee instead of a $75 barrier.

03 · Split the lapsed list in two.

Forty per cent cite cutting back, and the rest mostly just did not get around to it. One message cannot serve both. The first group needs the low and non-alcoholic range, the second needs one tap to reorder.

Jewell Projects holds Harvey Norman contacts at franchisee level, plus a contact who has done 20,000-plus installs for Harvey Norman, JB Hi-Fi and The Good Guys.

Wholesale as a function

Bars have approached Tap That and it still has not converted. That is not a demand problem, it is the absence of follow-up and sales experience.

Stand it up properly: a full-timer or a commissioned agent, targets and KPIs, a pipeline that lives in the CRM rather than in someone's head.

Then go after the shape of it: custom systems for sporting clubs, club-branded kegs, minimum guarantees per season, and moving rigs from footy to cricket so they earn year-round.

And work around the duopoly. Contractual lock-in is not the same as satisfaction. Find the pain points in locked venues and build a packaged offer that fits alongside the contract, profit share included if that is what it takes.

One account is worth about ten households. This is where the leverage is.

Found by people, and by machines

Within six to twelve months a large share of web traffic will expect an answer from AI search rather than a list of links. The site has to be readable by both.

The basics that are missing: a real About page, an FAQ that answers the buying objections, reviews on the site rather than off it, and the awards page the Crafted win deserves.

Reviews are the cheapest asset here. They exist, they are strong, and they sit on platforms rather than on the site where a model can read them.

Then the category play: nobody owns beer at home on the Gold Coast in search. Owning it is cheaper now than it will ever be again.
