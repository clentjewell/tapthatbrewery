The strategy, on one line

Stop selling systems to strangers and start owning the installed base: flag who owns what, make membership the default, and turn the 116 owners who bought elsewhere into the primary acquisition lane.

Provisional. Advisory review, 27 Aug: Discover is now an input to the next step rather than the output, so this strategy is held until the business questions are answered – cost drivers, the taproom decision, and the range. See #20B.

Because: systems go out near cost and the margin is the refill · 56% of owners already switched to us unprompted · members leak at 18% against 44% · the cheapest customer in the model is one who already owns the hardware.

Customer
Primary lane · 56% of owners
The switcher

Owns a system bought elsewhere. Reached behaviourally, not demographically. Converted at the counter and by search – never by a hardware pitch.

Retention lane · 44% leakage
The non-member owner

Drifts rather than defects. Answered with a cadence prompt at 60 and 75 days and a membership argument in dollars – not a discount.

Growth lane · 43% take 3+ months
The considerer

Enters via giveaway, taproom or a mate’s place. Needs ninety days of answers – cost, space, partner buy-in – before the close lands.

Acquisition mix today: taproom 22% · Google 20% · referral 18% · social 16% · giveaway 10%.

Business Model
  • Hardware near cost, margin in refills – about $70 gross per keg, roughly 1.55 kegs per customer per month.
  • Lifetime gross profit – ~$2,100 residential, ~$21,840 for a venue account. A venue is worth ten households.
  • Membership is the retention product, not a discount scheme: $250/yr, $30 off each refill, break-even at 8.33.
  • The taproom is a funnel stage, not a P&L line – 22% of acquisition, and it loses money on its own.
  • Six acquisition engines weighted by the client’s own plan: competitor conversion 40%, referral 25%, Google 15%, homebrewer 10%, marketplace 5%, service 5%.

No P&L, COGS or CAC exists. Every unit figure here comes from the client; nothing is modelled by us.

Messaging

The architecture

Taste leads. Cost states itself. Connection keeps them. Earlier drafts treated cost as a closing-only message on the “dirty little secret” read – the census does not support hiding it at 2.75.

By stage

Cold – freshness, the brewery floor, the award. Considering – the maths, the footprint, her taps. Owner – what’s pouring now. Lapsing – a brewery update, never a reference to absence.

One live claim must be corrected before anything ships: “$2.34 a schooner” is member-only. Non-member is $2.98, and it is running against “$14 at the pub”.

Platform
  • Website – a “we fill any system” page, a live range page off the Kegged & Ready board, and a cost calculator.
  • Search – refill intent first (“keg refill Gold Coast”), Google Business kept current. Google is already 20% of acquisition unaided.
  • Social – Facebook weighted equal to Instagram: 54% name it primary and the base skews 41–60.
  • Email & SMS – six sequences on GoTab transaction data; the highest-return channel at near-zero media cost.
  • CRM – one record per customer keyed on days-since-last-keg, with the switcher flag as the field that matters most.

GoTab × Fishbowl integration date is outstanding. Every automation is specified with a manual interim path.

Gate 2 Decision
Not signed – and it cannot be, until CP1 clears.

What is settled

  • Refills are the business; hardware is the entry cost.
  • The switcher lane is the cheapest acquisition available.
  • Membership is a retention instrument, not a promotion.
  • Two channels only – Meta and Google search.

What is blocked

  • Membership price – every brief, page and poster waits on it.
  • Cost on or off the table – decides the whole messaging architecture.
  • Model A or B – decides where the budget goes.
  • Brand lock – no vector artwork, no typeface licences.

What signature unlocks

  • Deploy sprint 3: paid creative and lifecycle content.
  • The ten execution briefs move from draft to production.
  • Website build against a locked page strategy.
  • Brand guidelines finished as three artefacts, not one.
