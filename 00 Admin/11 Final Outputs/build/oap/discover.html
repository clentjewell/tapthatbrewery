The Belief

Tap That Brewery believes fresh beer shouldn’t stop at the brewery door – that what’s actually pouring belongs on tap at home, for anyone who wants it, whoever sold them the system.

Read in order: the belief leads, the evidence earns it, the position states it. Everything on this sheet is drawn from the client’s own census and business records, not our estimates.

Killer Questions : the five truths
  1. 116 of 206 owners (56%) bought their system somewhere else and refill here anyway – the largest and cheapest pool in the business, and it is not flagged in any system.
  2. Taste & freshness ranks first (2.19), cost second (2.75) – close enough that the Scaling Up plan’s ban on competing at all on price is a decision, not a given.
  3. Members leak at 18%, non-members at 44%. Membership is the strongest retention lever available and its price is on two contradictory posters in the venue right now.
  4. 91.7% know Tap Tokens; only 43.8% know the keg-system referral exists. Referral is already 18% of acquisition while most of the base doesn’t know the reward.
  5. NPS is roughly +80, with 86% promoters. The product is not the problem. Commercial architecture and follow-up are.

Offer

Keg refills across a 27-tap range, and the systems to pour them: 2-tap $975 through 6-tap $2,550, integrated from $1,750, the 5L mini at $225, and Benchy for the 4WD. Systems go out near cost. The margin is the refill – about $70 gross per keg.

Membership is the lock: Keg Crew at $250/yr takes $30 off every refill – break-even at 8.33 refills, not the six being quoted to staff.

Proof: Midnight in Tokyo took Brewer’s Choice at Crafted in the first year of entering. NPS ≈ +80.

The Position

For Gold Coast households who want beer worth pouring at home, Tap That is the brewery that fills any system with what’s genuinely fresh – because it’s brewed fifteen minutes away, not shipped as commodity.

Nobody on the Gold Coast owns this category in market or in search. The white space is real and unclaimed.

Evidence : what this is built on
  • Client business records – 206 systems in the database; ~116 bought elsewhere. This outranks the census, which reported 31% on a response-biased sample.
  • Corrections applied 20 Aug – 46 documents rewritten in place, not just flagged. The baseline correction alone nearly doubles the growth multiple to 1,000, from ~4.5× to ~9×.
  • Customer census – self-selected, so directional on proportions and reliable on rankings: drivers, channels, cycle, delivery, NPS.
  • Five client planning documents – Scaling Up, growth playbook, vision document, census findings, raw data. They contradict each other on the core metric.
  • One discovery session, one site visit, 27 photographs – pricing, signage, menus and the membership posters as they actually are.

Still missing: Square refill history, P&L, COGS, CAC and margin. No structured discovery questionnaire was ever run.

Six questions block CP1. All six are decisions, not research – one founders’ session clears them.

  • Membership price – two posters, two prices, both live in the venue.
  • “Active” defined – 45, 75 or 90 days. Recommendation: 75.
  • Model A or B – sell more systems, or convert the 116 who bought elsewhere.
  • Which plan is live – three documents, three core metrics.
  • Is cost on the table – the census says it nearly leads.
  • Competitor name – verify before any comparison content.
