The one thing everything is built on04 / 10

The read.

Sixty-five documents is the right depth to build from. It is not what gets read on a Tuesday morning. So here is the whole argument in one sentence — if you only take one thing from the set, take this.

Tap That is a refill subscription business being run with a hospitality venue's attention.

The engine

Systems go out at cost. All the margin is in the refills — roughly $200 a month, two kegs, per active customer. 220 actives today against a 1,000 target.

The drift

Eighteen months of attention went to the taproom, which loses money and was only ever meant to be the shopfront. It converts 20–30% of visitors — it is a funnel, not a profit centre.

The cheapest growth

Half your refill customers bought their hardware somewhere else and switched to you with no campaign at all. Winning the installed base is cheaper than creating new owners.

Everything downstream follows from that: switchers before new system buyers, lifecycle automation before paid spend, the taproom scored on systems sold rather than bar take, and cost kept as the closer rather than the opening line — because the buying is done on kudos and connection, and the savings justify it afterwards.

In the interest of being straight with you06 / 10

What I have not done.

01
Website design and development.
The website strategy, sitemap, page strategy and build brief are written. The site itself is not designed or built — and I have not audited your current one, so anything that depends on what is live today is marked as pending that review.
02
Brand identity artwork.
You already have a brand, and a good one. The logo brief is scoped as import-and-lock, not redesign: inventory the marks, produce the missing variants, run the trademark check, lock it. No new identity has been designed, and none should be without you asking.
03
Keyword research and paid media testing.
The SEO strategy names the territories and the content to build; the search volumes behind them are not yet validated. Paid media is written as a test protocol with kill criteria, not a campaign — no budgets are set, because the P&L does not support guessing at them.
04
Anything that needed data I do not have.
The census results, your unit-level P&L, and the GoTab and Fishbowl integration dates are all still outstanding. Rather than invent them, the documents state what must be true and mark the gap. Section seven lists all of it.

Everything else in the 3D Process is drafted. These four are the deliberate boundary of this work.

To get this signed off07 / 10

Nine open items. Three of them block sign-off.

These are the caveats that recur across the set. Closing the first three is the fastest route to CP1.

01
Your membership pricing contradicts itself.
There are two membership posters live in the venue right now: one says Keg Crew is $250 a year, the other $300 plus a $120 renewal, and the token bonuses differ too. This is the single most-cited blocker in the whole set — it gates the website's membership page, every brief and all collateral. It is also the one thing worth fixing this week regardless of anything else here.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: CP1, website, all collateral
02
The census data.
Fifty respondents, and I only have the headline figures quoted in the session. The full data validates the segment splits, the delivery pricing and the moderation story that answers the "I'll drink too much" objection.
Owner: Clent → Jules · Blocks: CP1, segment validation
03
The competitor across the road.
Your handwritten note reads "Aardvark and Arrow"; the transcript heard "Ardbach and Arrow". Every document that relies on the name flags it. One line from you settles it — along with anything you know about their pricing and how they actually operate.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: competitor content, comparison pages
04
GoTab and Fishbowl integration dates.
The lifecycle automation is the highest-leverage work in the entire strategy — replacing the manual monthly SMS with triggers at 60, 75 and 90 days. The CRM plan includes a manual-export interim so nothing waits, but the real build needs a date.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: CRM plan, EDM automation, lease-to-buy
05
P&L detail, by unit.
"Always red at the bottom" is the whole picture I have. Unit-level revenue and the taproom's actual loss would turn the business plan from a structure into a plan with numbers in it.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: business plan, budget setting
06
Three founder confirmations.
The timeframe you want the 1,000 target inside; how long the taproom's loss is acceptable while it works as a funnel; and whether the franchise ambition can be said out loud in PR.
Owner: Chris & Justin · Blocks: OKRs, PR plan, CP2
07
Brand source files.
Logo SVGs, the actual hex values for the green and the gold, and the fonts. The brand guidelines describe your identity accurately from photographs — the exact values are marked pending your files.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: brand guidelines, logo lock
08
One beer name.
The coolroom board reads "Bong Water IPA" in one place and "Bone Water" in another. Small, but it will end up in copy.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: menu and collateral copy
09
Giveaway cadence.
To fit both giveaway closes inside the plan year, the calendar compresses them to about three and a half months apart. Tell me if that is too tight and it moves.
Owner: Tap That · Blocks: campaign calendar, activation plan
