## Overview

- Marketing discovery session — Jewell Projects (Clent/Christy) getting deep context on the client's business to build a marketing strategy.
- The business sells home keg systems and keg refills, runs a taproom, and does some wholesale — core goal is reaching **1,000** active keg system owners (currently at **220**), which maps to a ~**$2.6M** business at ~**$200/month** per customer.
- Keg refills and keg system sales are the heart of the business; the taproom is useful but draining, and wholesale is a secondary channel that ultimately feeds refill volume.
- The team knows their strategy and segments well — the gap is execution and resources, not ideas.
- Key near-term opportunities flagged: pre-existing keg system owners switching to them for refills, sports club partnerships, the "benchy" portable tap product, and better-automated re-engagement of dormant customers.
- Long-term vision is to franchise the model — owner has prior franchise experience through Back in Motion and sees a replicable, scalable model here.

## Team setup and roles

- Harry handles all organic social media, content ideas, and events/giveaway promotion, and also covers bar shifts (~70% marketing, 30% bar coverage).
- The hybrid role means bar coverage only kicks in when needed — if the venue is full, a venue manager comes in instead.

## Business vision and goals

- Short-term target: **1,000 active keg system owners or resellers** — up from **220** currently.
- At 1,000 customers averaging 2 kegs/month at ~$200/month spend, that's a ~**$2.6M** business.
- Long-term goal: franchise the model — owner has **10–12 years** in mind, with contacts from his Back in Motion franchise experience.
- The last **18 months** got pulled toward growing the taproom at the expense of the original focus on keg systems and refills — the team feels clear on strategy and needs execution support more than strategic direction.
- The business is currently running at a loss ("always red at the bottom of the P&L").

- Segment-specific campaigns haven't been built out yet despite the team knowing the framework.

## Business model and revenue streams

- Four business units: keg system sales, keg refills, taproom, wholesale.
- Keg systems are sold at cost (same price as buying direct from supplier) — margin comes from refills, not hardware.
- Kegs have strong margin; taproom margins are healthy at volume but the venue runs at a loss overall.
- ~**50%** of refill customers bought their system elsewhere and switched to them for fills — a meaningful acquisition channel.
- **20–30%** of taproom visitors end up buying a keg system after seeing it in person — the taproom functions as a funnel even if it's not profitable on its own.
- Owner's mental model: keg system sales → refills; wholesale → refills; taproom → keg system ownership → refills. Everything feeds refill volume.

## Keg products, pricing, and competitors

- Two keg types: **plastic kegs** (proprietary bladder system, pick up and go, no CO2 needed) and **corny kegs** (stainless, drop off Monday, pick up Friday). ~**70%** of pre-existing corny keg customers have switched to plastic for convenience.
- Keg size is **20L** (vs. industry standard 50L) — freshness and variety are key advantages.
- Pricing: **$120–$170** per keg vs. competitors at $85–$90; positioned as better quality and more consistent.
- Two-tap keg system sells for **$975** — sold at cost, no margin.
- No excise tax (under the threshold), no wholesaler margin — passes those savings to customers; a 50L keg of Balter from a wholesaler runs ~$500 vs. their ~$275 equivalent.
- Local competitors: "Ardbach and Arrow" *(per Circleback transcription; the handwritten workshop note reads "Aardvark and Arrow — across the road")* and Laughing Lizard (north end of the Gold Coast).
- Online/national competitors: Kegs Off Tap, Keg Republic — wholesale distributors.

## Buying cycle and purchase objections

- Long buying cycle — **60%** of buyers take up to 3 months to pull the trigger, **40%** take longer; only ~**10%** buy within a month.
- Four recurring objections: upfront cost (~$975), "my wife won't let me," nowhere to put it, and "I'll drink too much."
- Census of **50 respondents** found **70%** use their system daily or most days but only go through **1–2 kegs/month** — counters the over-drinking concern.
- Responses to objections: lease-to-buy option in development (pending GoTab POS capability, expected within a couple of months), RTDs and cocktails on tap for partner buy-in, and the "put a lock on the fridge" suggestion for self-control concerns.
- Keg system giveaway competitions (**2x/year**, ~1,000 entries each) are the primary driver of system sales — followed by a **50% discount** offer at competition close, which converted **22–25 purchases** last time.

## Taproom events and functions

- Ticketed music events (reggae nights, Brazilian nights, "duffs") work well — organizers take the door, the venue takes the bar; one recent event did a week's trade in 5 hours.
- A Brazilian night is booked for early September 2026.
- Private functions use minimum spends rather than a hire fee — benchmarked at **1 drink per person per hour**; only failed to hit minimum spend once.
- Capacity rough guide: mezzanine ~40, downstairs ~70, full venue ~120–130 seated; standing events push higher (109 recently, targeting 250 next).
- Food is the main friction point for private bookings — no kitchen or food license; food trucks require guaranteed numbers and are hard to secure.
- The venue wants more local business workers after work — industrial area, many nearby businesses, limited foot traffic otherwise.

## Wholesale outreach

- Walk-ins to Broadbeach restaurants failed — couldn't reach decision-makers or showcase the range. Cold email/calls had the same problem.
- Shifted to targeted matching: Japanese lager → Japanese/Asian fusion restaurants, Mexican lager → Mexican restaurants, craft range → US-style craft beer bars.
- Social media DMs got the most responses — venues would pass on a manager's contact; follow-up on those leads wasn't as tight as it could have been.
- Sports clubs are a strong opportunity but hit three objections: no packaged product (cans), existing supplier contracts, no tap infrastructure. NRL-affiliated venues are locked out via CUB exclusivity deals.

## Growth opportunities

- **Pre-existing keg system owners** are the lowest-hanging fruit for refill growth — many have had systems for years and haven't switched suppliers.
- **"Benchy" portable tap unit** (from Kegland) targets 4WD, camping, boating, caravan markets — no direct competitors currently; Harry's cousin runs a 4WD fit-out business and confirmed client interest.
- **Outdoor living partnerships** (pool builders, deck builders, architects, display homes, BBQ retailers) reach buyers 6–9 months before purchase-ready — existing collab with Smoked Barbecue Co (Father's Day keg system + smoking masterclass voucher) as proof of concept.
- **Sports club model**: supply the system, white-label the beer (e.g. "Surfers Paradise Demons Lager"), 10% discount to players + 10% kickback to the club.
- **UGC from real customers** (not the owners on camera) and a better-promoted referral program — both identified but underexecuted.
- **Delivery** is viable — census said 3–7 days wait and $20–$30 fee acceptable; GoTab integrates with Uber Direct for same-day delivery.
- **Venue hire to PR agencies** for client launch activations — untapped.
- **Wedding venues and function spaces** as wholesale targets for white-label kegs.

## Dormant customers and re-engagement

- Active = keg purchase within **75 days**; at-risk = **75–90 days**; churned = **90+ days**.
- Current re-engagement: manual monthly SMS to churned customers offering **$20 off** if they buy within 2 weeks — this month testing a free family-sized Marie's Pizza offer via a Buttercard partnership.
- Goal is to automate based on buying behavior once GoTab/Fishbowl integration is complete.
- Most churned customers personally contacted said they'd cut back on drinking or hadn't gotten around to coming in — lapsed, not lost.

## Product range and brand

- **27 taps** total — roughly 1/3 non-beer (RTDs, cocktails, ginger beer, seltzers, margaritas) — intentionally designed for partner/spouse buy-in.
- **"Midnight in Tokyo"** (black Japanese lager) won Brewer's Choice Award at Crafted — described as Australia's biggest beer festival — in their first year entering.
- Beers are gluten-reduced (can't legally claim gluten-free); the range skews lower-carb by design — health-conscious angle exists but isn't yet marketed.
- Naming leans into puns for sours ("Skittle Me This," "Slice Spice Baby," "Bone Water IPA") — more playful than the core lager names.
- The brand's "why," in the owner's words: **they're in the business of connection and good times — keg systems and refills are the means, not the end.**
