Traditional Media Rollout

Pre-CP1/CP2 draft — Deploy runbooks are written ahead of both gates. Nothing here should be executed until the Design plan it descends from is approved.

The position

Paid traditional media is held until the $1,500/month Meta programme (#68) has survived two full giveaway cycles against its own kill criteria. That is a decision, not a hedge: the business cannot yet attribute a walk-in to a message, and buying broadcast reach before the measurable channels are proven repeats the taproom mistake in media form.

What ships in the meantime is infrastructure — physical assets with a one-off cost and no ongoing spend. Those are not media buys and they are not held.

Ships now — infrastructure

1. Signage and wayfinding (highest priority in this document)

The venue's own diagnosis is an industrial precinct with limited foot traffic, and the taproom is nonetheless the single largest acquisition channel at 22%. Every person who can't find it is a lost acquisition at the top of the best-converting funnel there is.

Design rule: the sign must say "keg refills — any system", not only the brewery name. Google is 20% of acquisition and the search behaviour is refill-led; the physical sign should answer the same query.

Ships on a trigger

3. Letterbox drop — only when delivery is live

Delivery has real demand: 51% interested overall, 79% among stock-up buyers versus 40% among top-up buyers, with fee tolerance mostly $20–30. That makes a suburb-by-suburb service announcement the one message where a physical drop beats a feed impression.

Gate Requirement
Delivery licensed and operating Blocking
Suburb selected from GoTab postcode data Start with 4213 — the largest cluster
Unique offer code per suburb Non-negotiable; this is the only reason the drop is testable
Head-to-head design One test suburb by drop, one matched suburb by Meta geo. Keep the winner
Scale rule No second suburb until the first has a 90-day read — the buying cycle is 3+ months for 43%

Targeting rule from the census: the drop message leads with delivery, not with systems, and is aimed at the stock-up profile. A top-up household reading a delivery ad is a wasted impression at 40% interest.

4. Print advertorial — only after earned coverage

Advertorial amplifies a story that already ran; it never substitutes for one. Sequence: #71 lands the earned placement → assess whether a giveaway cycle needs incremental local reach that Meta entry volumes say it isn't getting → only then request a rate card. Same journalist relationship, same publication.

5. Local radio — the last item, and the easiest to waste

Gated on both giveaway cycles being measured on Meta. Test only if entry volume has plateaued and projected cost per entry beats Meta's actual. Preferred entry is a contra or prize partnership with the station, not a rate-card schedule. Note the audience fit is better than it looks — the base skews 41–60 — but "better than it looks" is not a reason to spend before the measurable channel is proven.

Explicitly not running

Television, billboards, cinema, general-awareness print schedules, always-on radio. Revisit only after profitability or a funded growth stage.

The comparison discipline

Every traditional proposal is written on one line against its digital equivalent before approval — radio against Meta giveaway amplification on cost per entry, letterbox against a matched Meta suburb, advertorial against boosted PR content. If the line can't be written, the buy isn't ready.

Measurement hooks

Signage: "how did you find us?" counter log, 8 weeks either side. Livery: unattributable by design — judged on cost, not response. Letterbox: unique code redemptions against the matched Meta suburb. Advertorial and radio: coded URL or offer only.

Stop conditions

Any paid buy proposed before #68 has two measured cycles · any drop without a unique code · any asset showing consumption or vehicle adjacency.
