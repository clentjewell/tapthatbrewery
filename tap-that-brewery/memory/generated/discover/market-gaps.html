Market Gaps

Evidence note (19 Aug 2026). Figures in this draft predate the client's census and planning documents. Several have been corrected; some interpretations are still under revision. Read 20A Evidence Reconciliation before citing anything here.

Purpose

Five gaps surfaced in discovery that no competitor — local, online, or retail — currently owns. For each: the evidence it exists, the signal of its size, and what claiming it would actually take. These are opportunity hypotheses for Design-phase prioritisation, not commitments.

Gap 1 — The installed-base refill relationship ("we fill any system")

The category's structural blind spot. Every competitor sells kegs; nobody owns the relationship with the Gold Coast's existing keg system owners — the garages and backyards that need filling monthly regardless of who sold the hardware. This is the white space D06 made quotable: nobody owns "beer on tap at home" on the Gold Coast.
Evidence ~56% of the 206 keg system owners in the database bought their system elsewhere and switched — with zero targeted campaign. ~70% of corny-keg holdouts converted to the plastic bladder system once they experienced it.
Size signal If half the current base arrived organically, the addressable installed base is plausibly several multiples of 220. It is the discovery session's stated "lowest-hanging fruit," and D06's H1 hypothesises switchers beat new-owner acquisition on CAC — no $975 objection, no 3-month buying cycle, margin from the first fill.
What claiming it takes A named "we fill any system" campaign lane (currently the offer exists but is unspoken); a first-fill switch incentive (Tap Tokens or member-rate first keg); corny Tue→Fri turnaround as the service proof; landing page + Meta/local targeting at homebrew and keg-owner audiences. Low capex — the constraint is Raef's ~70% marketing allocation.

Gap 2 — The portable/outdoor tap category (Benchy — zero competitors)
Evidence The Benchy portable unit (Kegland hardware) targets 4WD, camping, boating and caravan use — discovery's flat statement: no direct competitors currently. Raef's cousin runs a 4WD fit-out business and has confirmed client interest. The Smoked Barbecue Co Father's Day collab already proves the adjacent outdoor-living partnership model.
Size signal Qualitative only (one channel contact, no sales data) — but category creation, not share-stealing: 4WD/outdoor retail reaches buyers with money for lifestyle rigs, 6–9 months ahead of purchase intent, in channels no keg rival occupies. Every Benchy is a new refill account in a segment competitors can't see.
What claiming it takes Productise it (pricing, bundle, name it on the price card — currently absent); one flagship fit-out partnership via the existing 4WD contact; content built for the segment (rig installs, campsite pours — UGC-native); a decision on refill logistics for customers who roam (5L format and delivery both help). Modest effort, but it needs an owner or it stays an anecdote.

Gap 3 — The health-conscious range (gluten-reduced, lower-carb — unmarketed)
Evidence The beers are gluten-reduced (cannot legally claim gluten-free) and the range skews lower-carb by design; discovery confirms the angle exists but isn't yet marketed — it appears nowhere in venue signage or socials. The census (n=50) shows the customer is a moderator, not a binger: 70% use their system daily/most days yet drink only 1–~1.55 kegs/month (census).
Size signal D06's H7 flags it as a differentiator for the female market (the veto-holder on the #1 objection, "my wife won't let me") and moderating drinkers. No local keg rival or distributor claims health credentials — a first-mover claim that costs copy, not capex.
What claiming it takes Compliance first: lock the permissible wording ("gluten-reduced," carb claims) before any copy ships. Then: tap decals/menu flags, a website range page, and the "fresher, better, not more" moderation frame from D02. Cheapest gap on this list to claim — and the easiest for a competitor to copy once spoken, so speed matters.

Gap 4 — White-label club and wedding beer
Evidence The model is already designed: supply the system, white-label the beer ("Surfers Paradise Demons Lager"), 10% player discount + 10% club kickback. Wedding and function venues named in discovery as white-label wholesale targets. Sports clubs currently rebuff cans-and-contracts pitches (no packaged product, existing supplier deals, no tap infrastructure); NRL-affiliated venues are CUB-locked.
Size signal Every club or venue converted is a standing multi-keg refill account, not a one-off — wholesale exists to feed refill volume. The 10%/10% structure converts sponsorship-fatigued clubs by making the club a stakeholder, an offer neither CUB nor the online distributors is structured to make.
What claiming it takes A one-page club offer (mock decal, the 10%/10% maths); target only non-affiliated clubs with tap infrastructure or bundle the system in; fix the follow-up leak D06 names — DM outreach gets responses but pipeline dies between first reply and manager contact. Needs a tracked pipeline more than it needs marketing.

Gap 5 — Home delivery
Evidence Census-validated demand: respondents accept a $20–30 fee and 3–7 day wait. GoTab integrates with Uber Direct for same-day. No local keg rival delivers; online distributors ship freight, not fresh.
Size signal Convenience is a stated brand pillar the current model under-delivers (pickup only, corny turnaround excepted). Delivery widens the geographic refill radius beyond Burleigh, extends keg cadence for at-risk customers (75–90 days) who "hadn't gotten around to coming in," and is the fulfilment layer Gaps 1 and 2 both lean on.
What claiming it takes GoTab × Uber Direct integration switched on (pending); a priced pilot ($20–30 per census); a delivery radius decision; positioning as fresh, local, to your door — the one claim warehouse distributors cannot make. Blocked on the same integration backlog as lifecycle automation — sequencing decision for Design.

Priority read (proposed)

Gap 1 is the strategy — cheapest CAC, direct line to the 1,000-customer target. Gap 3 is the fastest claim (copy only, compliance permitting). Gaps 4 and 5 are gated by pipeline discipline and the GoTab integration respectively. Gap 2 is the defensible long game: the only gap where Tap That Brewery wouldn't just lead the category but be the category.

Assumptions & open items

  • All size signals are directional — no market sizing exists for GC keg-system installed base, 4WD accessory demand, or club venue counts.
  • Census figures are headline-only (n=50, full data pending); delivery fee tolerance rests on it.
  • Gluten-reduced/low-carb claims need compliance wording locked before use.
  • Benchy pricing and margin unconfirmed; competitor absence is the client's claim, not a verified scan.
  • Competitor name "Aardvark & Arrow" unverified (transcript: "Ardbach and Arrow").
