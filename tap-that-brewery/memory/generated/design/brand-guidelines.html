How to read this document

Tap That Brewery already has an identity — it's on the tap decals, the price board, the happy hour sign and the shirts behind the bar. These guidelines document what exists so everything new matches it; they do not reinvent anything. 1. Logo and marks (as observed in venue)

Mark Where seen Use
Tap That Brewery badge (primary logo — brewery name lockup) Signage, price board, flyers, merch wall The default mark on anything customer-facing
TT mark (compact monogram) Tap decals, small-format applications Where the full badge won't fit: taps, glassware, favicons
Wordmark / name-in-type Menu screens, receipts, documents Text settings where a graphic mark is impractical

Open decision — the name on the mark. These documents call the business Tap That Brewery, but the badge artwork reads TAP THAT · BREWERY & KEGHOUSE, as does the in-venue signage suite. Two ways to resolve it, and it is the founders' call: (a) treat "Tap That Brewery" as the short/spoken name and keep the full lockup as the registered mark — the usual and cheapest answer; or (b) retire "& Keghouse", which means new artwork and re-signing the venue. Nothing here assumes (b). Until it is decided, never redraw or crop the badge to remove "& Keghouse" — use the mark exactly as supplied.

2. Colour

The observed identity is green and gold — brewery green as the ground, gold as the accent (consistent with the in-venue signage suite and decals; the pairing also reads unmistakably Australian).

Accent Gold #CE9A49
Support Off-white (logo lettering) #DFDFDF
Support Charcoal/black (menu boards, Midnight in Tokyo styling) [TBC — not present in the logo artwork]

Source of these values. Sampled directly from the primary badge artwork (TT-Primary-Logo-2 / logo.svg, 3806 × 2557px, transparent) published on tapthatbrewery.com.au — not from site photos, whose colour venue lighting shifts. Confirm against the original vector files when they arrive; the wordmark and secondary marks have not been sampled.

Accessibility note. The brand gold #CE9A49 reads well on the brand green (5.30:1) but fails on light grounds (2.27:1 on off-white) — too low for body or small text. For gold text on a light background use a darkened tint of the same hue, #8A5F22 (5.07:1). This is a legibility tint, not a second brand colour, and should never appear on a dark ground where the true gold belongs.

Standing rules: green leads, gold accents — never the reverse; no additional brand colours introduced.

Observed style: bold condensed sans-serif headlines (price board, happy hour and signage suite), with a simpler workhorse sans for menu/body settings. 4. Voice & tone

The voice is already working in venue. Two registers, one brand (see 31 — Regular Guy × Jester):

Cheeky (the default) — puns that land at exactly the level the trading name sets, never cruder: - "Tap That Knockoffs" — happy hour, Mon–Fri 3–5pm - "Skittle Me This", "Slice Spice Baby", "Bone Water IPA" — sour/limited naming - "Peckish? / Ravenous?" — the food flip-sign

Straight-up generous (when it matters) — plain, warm, zero spin: - "5 ways to support us" receipt sign — review, mailing list, follow, engage, word of mouth — total $0.00 - Corny keg promise: drop by COB Tuesday, ready Friday - Social responsibility statement (drink-driving and DFV program support) — always straight, never joked about

Rules: puns for names and moments, plain talk for money, service and responsibility. Australian, first-person plural ("we"), talks with drinkers not at them. Never crude beyond the name's own wink; never beer-snob jargon; never discount-shouty.

5. Photography

Direction observed and to be continued: real venue, real customers, no stock gloss. - Shoot the working brewhouse (fermenters and corny kegs are the backdrop), festoon-lit taproom, the tap wall, the pour, real installs in real garages/sheds/backyards. - People: actual customers and staff — UGC from real owners over founders-on-camera (per discovery). - Never: stock models, studio-perfect product shots, anything that couldn't have been taken at Burleigh Heads or a customer's place.

6. Applications (as practised in venue)

  • Signage: framed, consistent suite (price board, happy hour, referral rewards) — new signs match the existing frame/format system.
  • Tap decals: every beer gets its own decal with the TT mark system; decal holder is even sold as an accessory ($25) — customer taps carry the brand home.
  • Socials (@tapthatbreweryandkeghouse): venue voice carried online — pun-led captions, real photos, Buffer-scheduled.
  • Merch: tees, caps, growlers, Tap That glass ($14) — badge or TT mark, green/gold only.
  • Docs/menus: wordmark + workhorse sans; prices always current (see gap below).

7. Don'ts

  1. Don't redraw or restyle any logo mark — import and lock (36).
  2. Don't fight on sticker price in any branded material — per-litre maths only, and only in closing contexts.
  3. Don't let the brand skew "dads only" — the 27-tap, third-non-beer range is core identity; show it.
  4. Don't use binge cues — the brand line is fresher and better, never more (census: 70% of daily users pour 1–~1.55 kegs/month (census)).
  5. Don't joke about responsibility, safety or money.
  6. Don't run conflicting collateral — the two live membership posters ($250/yr vs $300+$120 renewal) must be reconciled and one withdrawn.
  7. Don't add colours, fonts or sub-brands without a guidelines update.
