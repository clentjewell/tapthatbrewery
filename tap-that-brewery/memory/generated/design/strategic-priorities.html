Strategic Priorities

Pre-CP1 draft – produced ahead of Discover sign-off; census data, membership pricing reconciliation and competitor verification may revise inputs.

Discovery’s core finding: the constraint is execution capacity, not strategy – one ~70% marketer cannot run segment campaigns, wholesale outreach, automation, UGC and events at once. These priorities exist to sequence brutally. Ranked; each states why now, what changes, and what we stop doing.

P1 – Refill acquisition, switchers first

Why now. 116 of 206 owners (56%) bought their hardware elsewhere and switched with zero targeted effort – the cheapest acquisition available (no $975 hardware giveaway required, no 3-month buying cycle). Nobody on the Gold Coast owns the installed base of existing keg systems; Aardvark & Arrow and Laughing Lizard sell kegs, not the relationship. What changes. A standing "we fill any system" campaign lane: dedicated landing page, Meta targeting of keg-owner behaviours, referral hook (500 Tap Tokens per refill referral already exists on the wall – put it online). First-fill offer priced against the $85–90 local kegs without discounting the range (H1 test). What we stop.* Treating every new active as someone who must first be sold a system.

P2 – Lifecycle automation before new-audience spend

Why now. Churn management is a hand-sent monthly SMS ($20-off/2-week window). With active <75 days / at-risk 75–90 / churned 90+ already defined, and most churned customers "lapsed, not lost," the fix is mechanical – but blocked on GoTab/Fishbowl integration. Every month of delay leaks refill revenue from a list the business already owns. What changes. Push the integration to done; then automated touches at 60/75/90 days (pre-churn nudge before the window closes – H4), giveaway-entrant nurture matched to the 3-month buying cycle (H3), and winback offers (incl. the Marie’s Pizza/Buttercard test) triggered, not remembered. What we stop. Manual monthly SMS as the retention system; spending on cold reach while the warm list leaks.

P3 – Re-weight effort from taproom to engine

Why now. The last 18 months drifted to the venue; it loses money and was only ever the shopfront. It converts 20–30% [unverified – see #20B] of visitors to system owners – that is its job and its budget line. What changes. Taproom marketing is scoped to what feeds conversion: events that fill the room (ticketed nights already do a week’s trade in 5 hours), the demo moment, giveaway entry capture. Harry’s 70% marketing time is allocated engine-first (P1/P2) with a fixed, small taproom quota. Taproom→system conversion becomes a tracked number, not folklore. What we stop. Venue-first content calendars; founder and marketer attention defaulting to whatever is visible and social.

P4 – Channel discipline: wholesale as white-label opportunism only

Why now. Cold walk-ins and emails to Broadbeach restaurants failed; DM leads got responses but follow-up was loose; NRL-affiliated clubs are CUB-locked. Wholesale earns effort only where it is differentiated: white-label ("Surfers Paradise Demons Lager", 10% player discount / 10% club kickback – H6), style-matched venues, weddings/functions. What changes. A small, named target list; a simple follow-up cadence for DM-sourced leads; everything else declined. What we stop. Broad wholesale prospecting; chasing CUB-locked venues.

P5 – Collateral and pricing hygiene

Why now. Two live membership posters disagree ($250/yr vs $300 + $120/yr renewal, different token values); the tasting paddle is $18 on the sign and $20 on screen. Trivial in-venue; fatal online – no funnel, pricing page or membership pitch can be built on unreconciled numbers, and it erodes the "consistent quality" positioning that justifies $120–170 kegs against $85–90 rivals. What changes. One price list, reconciled and dated, as the single source for all Design-phase copy; old poster removed. What we stop. Publishing anything customer-facing before reconciliation.

P6 – Measure the engine, not the noise

Why now. The $2.6M target decomposes into one number – active keg customers – yet today’s evidenced measurement is a leaderboard photo and headline census figures. A team of Harry plus two founders cannot act on a dashboard they don’t have. What changes. #26 Measurement Plan implemented: active count weekly, the supporting funnel monthly, sourced from GoTab/Fishbowl/Buffer. What we stop. Judging marketing by taproom busyness and follower counts.

Assumptions & open items

  • Ranking assumes GoTab/Fishbowl integration is achievable near-term – timeline unconfirmed; if it slips past Stage 1, P2 tactics run semi-manually off exported lists.
  • Switcher-pool size (existing GC keg owners) is inferred, not counted.
  • *Aardvark & Arrow spelling unverified (Circleback heard "Ardbach and Arrow").
  • P3 assumes founders accept a capped taproom remit – needs explicit sign-off at CP1.
