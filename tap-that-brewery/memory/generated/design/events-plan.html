Events Plan

Pre-CP1 draft — produced ahead of Discover sign-off; census data, membership pricing reconciliation and competitor verification may revise inputs.

The rule that governs this plan

The taproom loses money and already swallowed 18 months of attention. Events are how it earns its keep — but only if every event is judged as a system-sales funnel stage, not as bar revenue. Discovery's own number sets the bar: 20–30% of taproom visitors go on to buy a keg system. An event that packs the room with people who will never own a system is a good night and a bad allocation. So: every event in this plan carries a refill-funnel scorecard (below), and the plan's total cost is essentially operational — no event here requires a marketing budget beyond organic social and EDM invitations [external spend TBC only if a specific event warrants boosting via #46].

Event formats (the portfolio)

1. Ticketed music nights — the proven cash engine

The model is already validated: organiser takes the door, venue takes the bar; reggae nights, Brazilian nights, "duffs" — one recent event did a week's trade in 5 hours. A Brazilian night is booked for early September. Standing recently hit 109; the team wants 250.

  • Cadence: 1–2 per month, organiser-led (their audience, their promotion — the venue's marketing cost is near zero).
  • Funnel duty: these rooms are full of non-customers — the highest-value capture moment the venue gets. Every music night runs the giveaway-entry mechanic at the door or bar (QR entry = the hand-raise that starts the 90-day nurture, #47 seq 1) and the tap wall does the demo work.
  • Watch-out: 250 standing needs a licensing/capacity check [TBC], and door-model contracts should be templated once, not renegotiated per organiser.

2. Members' tasting nights — the retention play

Already a published Keg Crew/Mug Club perk (tasting-night invites + a free drink, per the membership poster). Run quarterly off the Kegged & Ready pipeline (Tropical Hazy, Smuggler's Haze, Espresso Martini…): Chris presents, members taste what's coming, and pre-orders/keg reservations are taken on the night.

  • Funnel duty: cadence protection for the highest-value list (#48 high-value view), renewal ammunition (#47 seq 6), and the venue for journalist hospitality (#49). Members bring a plus-one — the warmest possible prospect.

3. Demo days — the conversion play

The "that's a keg system?" moment, manufactured. Monthly Saturday session: systems pouring, the 5L mini ($225) as the impulse exit, Afterpay/Zip signage, the census moderation stat on display for the veto-holder, and the full non-beer wall poured for partners. Timed to sit inside giveaway nurture windows (days 15–60, when entrants are negotiating at home) so EDM can drive entrants to a live demo.

  • Funnel duty: this is the only format whose primary metric is systems sold; everything else feeds it.

4. Private functions — the margin play with a food catch

Minimum-spend model (no hire fee), benchmarked at 1 drink per person per hour — missed only once. Capacity: mezzanine ~40, downstairs ~70, full venue ~120–130 seated.

  • The food constraint: no kitchen, no food licence; food trucks demand guaranteed numbers and are hard to secure. Workarounds, in order of effort: (a) formalise the Ammazza pizza arrangement into a per-head catering menu ($21 pizzas, kids packs, platters) quoted with every function; (b) build a two-or-three-deep roster of pre-vetted caterers/trucks with agreed minimums so "guaranteed numbers" is solved by booking size, not luck; (c) longer term, cost a food-licence pathway [TBC — regulatory + capex question, not this plan's call].
  • Funnel duty: every function guest is a taproom first-timer at someone else's party — the venue-hire sign already points to cheers@; functions get the same giveaway-QR treatment as music nights. Wedding/PR-agency venue hire (flagged in discovery as untapped) routes through the same package.

5. The "local business workers" weekday play — the idle-capacity play

The venue sits in an industrial estate with limited foot traffic and wants after-work locals. The assets already exist: Tap That Knockoffs happy hour, Mon–Fri 3–5pm (jugs $16/$20/$40) and the 22-foot shuffleboard.

  • Tactics, all near-zero cost: a physical walk of the estate with a Knockoffs flyer + first-jug offer for staff of named neighbouring businesses (the prospect list is literally the street); a standing "Friday knock-offs" booking offer for workplaces (mezzanine, minimum-spend-lite); shuffleboard ladder for local businesses. Workplaces are a segment (~5%) where one system sale serves many drinkers — the office keg system pitch is the natural upsell to a workplace that starts coming for Knockoffs.

The event scorecard (applied to every event, no exceptions)

Metric Source Why it ranks above bar take
Giveaway entries / new contacts captured QR entries tagged to the event (#48 provenance) Feeds the largest intent list
Demo interactions / demo-day bookings Staff tally The 20–30% conversion moment
Systems sold within 30 days, attributed GoTab + provenance field The actual product of the funnel
New memberships sold GoTab LTV engine attach
Bar take vs minimum spend GoTab Still counted — but fourth, not first
Staff/founder hours consumed Roster The scarcest input; guards against taproom gravity

Quarterly review: rank events by contacts captured and systems attributed per staff-hour. Kill criterion: any recurring format that, after three runs, captures no contacts and attributes no systems is either re-tooled to capture or dropped — regardless of how good the bar take was. That sentence is the whole defence against the last 18 months repeating.
