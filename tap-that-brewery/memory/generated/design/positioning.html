The positioning statement

Built from D03's one-sentence position, confirmed against the #10 category map, now formalised:

For Gold Coast households who'd rather host than go out, Tap That Brewery is the home-tap brand that keeps award-winning beer — and a 27-tap range for everyone else — flowing at your place, because it brews 20L kegs in Burleigh and fills any keg system, whoever sold it. Good times at yours beat a night out anywhere.

Compressed (for decks and briefs): The Gold Coast's home-tap brand — brewed in Burleigh, fills any system, good times at your place.

Frame of reference: the home-tap category, not the craft-brewery category

Tap That Brewery must refuse the frame it superficially fits. As a craft brewery it is one of dozens on the Gold Coast, across the road from a cheaper one. As the home-tap brand it is alone: nobody — not Aardvark & Arrow, not Laughing Lizard, not the online distributors — is building the relationship with the installed base of keg systems sitting in Gold Coast garages that need filling every month. The category claim is "beer on tap at home" (already the headline of Tap That Brewery's own in-venue flyer: "Did you say beer on tap at home?"*). The taproom, in this frame, is not the product — it is the demo floor, converting 20–30% of visitors into category entrants.

Points of parity (must match, never lead)

  • Keg refills readily available locally (parity with keg rivals — theirs is cheaper; ours is never argued on sticker).
  • Hardware access and payment ease (systems at cost, Afterpay/Zip; parity with online distributors on obtainability).
  • Convenience of pickup (Talos kegs, no CO2, grab-and-go — parity with a bottle-shop run).
  • Hospitality basics at the taproom (parity with local venues; the taproom does not need to out-pub the pubs).

The flank: "we fill any system"

The single most efficient move in the strategy. It converts every competitor's past hardware sale into Tap That Brewery's future refill customer — skipping the $975 objection, the partner veto and the 3-month buying cycle, straight to the ~$200/month margin engine. It costs the brand nothing (refill capacity exists), it cannot be copied without the range and freshness to back it, and it is already half-proven organically. This phrase gets owned: on the website, on the price board, in an always-on campaign lane ranked above every demographic campaign on cost-per-customer (H1).

Repositioning implications, per competitor type

Against Their position Tap That Brewery's move
Local keg-refill breweries (Aardvark & Arrow*, Laughing Lizard) Same model, cheaper ($85–90) Reposition them as the keg swap: one product, no range, no club. Tap That Brewery is the brand you belong to, not the counter you swap at. Price never mentioned.
Online distributors (Kegs Off Tap, Keg Republic) Commodity kegs from a warehouse Reposition them as freight: 50L of someone else's beer on a truck. Tap That Brewery brews what you pour, 20L at a time, five minutes away, with a door you can walk through.
Bottleshops / big retail Packaged convenience Reposition the slab as the warm compromise: per-litre maths plus the pour. This is the recruitment fight — most future customers currently shop here.
Pubs / venues The night out Reposition the night out as the expensive way to see your mates: babysitter, closing time, $12 schooners. Your place wins. Recruitment fight #2.
CUB-locked venues Contracted taps No repositioning — route around via white-label ("Surfers Paradise Demons Lager", 10% player / 10% club) and non-affiliated clubs, weddings, functions.

Structural note carried from #10: only the first row sells what Tap That Brewery sells. Rows three and four are where the customers are — positioning must recruit from retail and pubs while defending against the keg swap across the road. Two jobs, two message sets (33 Messaging handles the split).
