## Purpose

Six differentiators, each with proof already in hand – no aspiration, no "we believe." For each: the evidence, the contrast line it earns, and where it should show up (web, in-venue, campaigns). Input to DS04 Brand Strategy and all Design-phase copy; contrast lines are drafts for voice work, not final copy.

## The rule

A differentiator without proof is a slogan. Every entry below survives D03's test: backed by something photographed in venue, said by the owner, or counted in the data – not drawn so Tap That Brewery wins by definition.

## The six differentiators

### 1. Award-winning brewing

- **Proof:** Midnight in Tokyo (black Japanese lager, 4.6%) won Brewer’s Choice at Crafted – described as Australia’s biggest beer festival – in Tap That Brewery’s *first year entering*. Head brewer Chris Smith’s pedigree (ex Burleigh Barrels, 4 Pines venues) documented in the Gold Coast Bulletin founding article.
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike the keg swap across the road, the beer in your keg took Brewer’s Choice at Australia’s biggest beer festival – first year we entered.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – homepage trust bar + Midnight in Tokyo product page. In-venue – award signage at the tap and on the takeaway price board (currently absent). Campaigns – the justifier in every switcher ad; the answer to "why pay $120 when it’s $85 over there."

### 2. 20L freshness vs 50L commodity

- **Proof:** 20L brewed on site against the industry-standard 50L distribution format; the customer watches the fermenters from the bar rail. Smaller format means faster turnover and more variety per system.
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike the online keg warehouses, Tap That Brewery brews what you pour – 20L at a time, fresh, five minutes from where you drink it.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – "why 20L" explainer on the refills page. In-venue – the brewhouse *is* the display; coolroom "Kegged & Ready / Crafting Soon" board turned into content. Campaigns – the core anti-distributor message; "brewed this week" freshness-dating worth testing.

### 3. 27-tap range including non-beer

- **Proof:** 27 taps, ~1/3 deliberately non-beer – seltzers, RTDs, gin sours, cocktails, ginger beer (photo-verified tap wall). Engineered to defeat the #1 objection ("my wife won’t let me"): her drinks on tap too. Gluten-reduced, lower-carb range as unmarketed backup (see #11, Gap 3).
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike the cheap keg swap, Tap That Brewery is a range: 27 taps, a third of them for the people who don’t drink beer.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – full tap list with ABVs and non-beer filter. In-venue – already the hero; add "a third of these aren’t beer" framing. Campaigns – the partner-buy-in beat in every young-family and tradie ad ("and here’s what’s on tap for her").

### 4. The membership/Tap Tokens/leaderboard ecosystem

- **Proof:** Keg Crew membership ($250/yr: $30 off every refill, 100 tokens, 5L birthday keg – *pricing reconciliation pending, two conflicting posters*); Tap Tokens on every dollar with a public leaderboard (Jack P. leading on 7,569 points, 1/8/26); referral rewards (1,000 tokens per system referral, 500 per refill referral); giveaways drawing ~1,000 entries. Competitors run transactions; nobody else has a leaderboard.
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike a keg supplier, Tap That Brewery is a club you’re winning – tokens, leaderboard, birthday keg and all.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – membership page (post-reconciliation) + live leaderboard. In-venue – already strong; make Keg Crew the default at system purchase, not an upsell. Campaigns – retention and referral engine; leaderboard and token milestones as recurring social content.

### 5. Corny keg service: Tue drop → Fri ready

- **Proof:** Photo-verified sign: corny kegs dropped by COB Tuesday, cleaned ($3) and filled by Friday. A concrete service promise no warehouse shipper structurally can match – and the front door for the "we fill any system" claim (see #11, Gap 1), since corny owners are, by definition, people who bought hardware elsewhere.
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike the distributors, we don’t care whose gear you own – drop your keg Tuesday, pour our beer Friday.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – "we fill any system" page with the Tue→Fri promise as the mechanism. In-venue – existing sign, keep. Campaigns – the switcher campaign’s working proof point; the weekend framing ("ready for Friday") writes its own creative.

### 6. No-excise price-per-litre economics

- **Proof:** Under the excise threshold, no wholesaler margin: ~$275 equivalent vs ~$500 for a 50L Balter through a wholesaler. A 20L keg is 44 schooners for $120–170 against $10–18 a schooner at a venue.
- **Contrast line:** *Unlike the pub, your garage doesn’t have a closing time – or a babysitter surcharge. And the beer costs half as much a litre.*
- **Where it shows up:** Web – a savings calculator on the refills page (kegs/month → dollars vs bottle-o and pub). In-venue – visible, not hidden: cost ranks **2.75**, second only to taste at **2.19**, so the old instinct to keep it off the wall is not supported. Campaigns – cost runs in the open feed alongside taste, rather than waiting for retargeting.
