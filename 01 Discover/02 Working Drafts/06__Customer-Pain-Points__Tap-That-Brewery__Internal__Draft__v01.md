## Why this document exists

Copy that names the customer's pain in the customer's words outperforms copy that describes the product. This document banks the pains per segment (pre-purchase) and per lifecycle stage (post-purchase) so Design-phase creative pulls lines from here rather than inventing them. Phrases in quotes are the voice of the customer — drawn from the discovery session's stated objections and segment logic, written the way the segment would say them.

## Pre-purchase pains by segment

| Segment | The pain, in their words | Underneath it | Pillar that answers it |
|---|---|---|---|
| Young families | "Going out is dead — by the time we've paid a sitter and Ubers, it's $300 before the first beer." | Social life must relocate to the backyard, but hosting well is effortful and expensive — warm slabs, ice runs, esky clutter | Connection (cost closes) |
| Young families | "We host every weekend and I spend Saturday morning at the bottle-o." | Hosting logistics tax; nothing for the partners to drink but wine | Convenience; the non-beer taps |
| High-volume drinkers | "My bottle-shop spend is embarrassing — $100+ every weekend and it's still warm tins." | Volume economics: pub prices worse, packaged beer stale next to fresh 20L | Cost + convenience |
| Cashed-up tradies | "The shed's 90% done — has been for two years." | The man-cave/outdoor build is a status project with no crown; a fridge of tins impresses nobody | Kudos |
| Cashed-up tradies | "Mates come round and I'm handing out cans like it's a worksite." | Hosting doesn't match the money spent on the build | Kudos + connection |
| Baby boomers | "When the family's over I want it done properly, without me running around all afternoon." | Generous hosting is identity; fuss and mediocre beer undermine it | Connection; taste/freshness |
| Share houses | "Four of us, one fridge, everyone's beer goes missing." | Party utility without individual expense; splitting $975 four ways is easy | Kudos + cost |
| DINKs | "We'd rather two great drinks than a fridge of average ones." | Quality signalling; cocktails/top-shelf at home | Kudos + taste/freshness |
| Workplaces | "Friday knock-offs are a trip to the bottle-o and warm cups in the car park." | Team ritual with no infrastructure | Connection + convenience |
| Partner / veto-holder | "It's a thousand dollars for *his* beer." | Sees a beer toy, not a household asset; nothing in it for her | The 27-tap answer: ~1/3 non-beer — seltzers, gin fizz sour, ginger beer, cocktails on tap |
| Existing system owners (behavioural) | "I've got the kegerator — getting it filled is the annoying part." | Commodity swaps, no relationship, stale or inconsistent fills elsewhere | Taste/freshness + convenience ("we fill any system") |

Two pains recur across every segment and belong in all creative: **the night out no longer stacks up** (cost, logistics, babysitters, closing time) and **hosting at home is effortful** — the keg system converts hosting from a chore into a flex.

## The four purchase-blocking pains (objections)

Named in discovery as the recurring four; treated fully in document 07:

1. "**A grand up front** is a lot for beer." (~$975)
2. "**My wife won't let me.**" (the veto-holder overlay)
3. "**Nowhere to put it.**" (space, rental, bench real estate)
4. "**I don't trust myself — I'll drink too much.**" (countered by census: 70% of daily users go through only 1–~1.55 kegs/month (census))

## Pains of the refill lifecycle (post-purchase)

The margin lives here, so friction here is churn. Lifecycle definitions in use: active <75 days since last keg, at-risk 75–90, churned 90+.

| Lifecycle pain | In their words | Consequence | Current / pending answer |
|---|---|---|---|
| Running dry | "Mates arrived Saturday and the keg blew halfway through." | The system's whole promise fails at the social moment it exists for | No low-keg warning today; delivery (GoTab × Uber Direct, same-day capable) and cadence-based prompts post Fishbowl integration |
| Forgetting to swap | "I keep meaning to drop in — it's been two months." | The lapsed-not-lost drift: churned customers contacted said they'd "not gotten around to it" | Manual monthly SMS ($20 off, 2-week window); automation at 60/75/90 days pending GoTab/Fishbowl |
| The refill errand | "Burleigh's a 25-minute round trip with the empty rattling in the boot." | Convenience pillar erodes with distance from the taproom | Census says $20–30 delivery fee and 3–7 days acceptable; Uber Direct beats that |
| Corny keg wait | "Drop it Tuesday, get it Friday — so I'm dry half the week." | Stainless owners lose days per cycle (+ $3 cleaning fee) | The Talos kegs: pick-up-and-go, no CO2 — ~70% of corny customers have already switched |
| Range paralysis / same-again fatigue | "I just get the lager because I don't know what's pouring." | Refill cadence slows when the range isn't visible at home | Tasting notes QR exists in venue; no owned digital "what's kegged & ready" channel yet |
| Membership ambiguity | "Is it $250 a year or $300? What do I actually get?" | Two conflicting live posters blunt the strongest retention offer ($30 off every refill) | Reconciliation flagged as a production gate in D06 |

## Assumptions & open items

- Quoted lines are Jewell renderings of client-stated pains, not verbatim customer quotes — validate and harvest real wording via the pending census detail and refill-counter conversations.
- "Running dry" and "range fatigue" are inferred lifecycle pains; the confirmed churn narrative is "cut back on drinking" or "hadn't gotten around to it."
- Distance/delivery pain assumes customer postcodes spread beyond Burleigh — confirm from sales data.
