Q. Is there a beverage, product, service, or feature you wish Tap That offered that we currently don't?

Key Finding

Responses were diverse, however three clear categories emerged:

1. More Approachable Beer Styles

The most common product request was not for additional craft beer styles.

Instead, respondents repeatedly requested:

Australian-style lagers

Mid-strength beers

Sessionable beers

Familiar mainstream beer styles

Customers specifically referenced:

VB

Asahi

Tooheys New

Aussie Lager

Mid-strength Lager

more frequently than any other beer category.

Strategic Implication

The survey suggests there may be an opportunity to develop a high-quality Australian-style lager or mid-strength offering that appeals to a broader audience.

This could potentially:

Attract prospective keg system owners who currently drink mainstream beers

Provide a familiar option for guests

Increase sessionability and consumption occasions

Reduce the perception that Tap That is exclusively for craft beer drinkers

2. RTDs, Spirits & Alternative Beverages

A surprisingly strong theme was the desire for beverages beyond beer.

Requests included:

Bourbon & Coke

Sugar-Free Bourbon & Coke

Gin & Tonic

Cocktails on Tap

RTDs

Strategic Implication

Customers are not necessarily looking to replace beer.

Instead, they are looking to:

Increase the number of people using the system

Cater for partners and guests

Provide greater beverage variety

Expand drinking occasions

This supports the broader census finding that system utilisation increases when customers have more beverage options available.

Opportunity

RTDs may represent one of the simplest ways to:

Increase household participation

Increase keg turnover

Create additional reasons to own multiple taps

3. Convenience & Ownership Services

Several respondents requested services rather than products.

Suggestions included:

Beer line cleaning

Line cleaning services

CO₂ bottle refills

General maintenance support

While these requests were not as frequent as beverage requests, they are notable because they appeared unprompted.

Strategic Implication

These responses reinforce one of the strongest themes from the entire census:

Customers are not frustrated with Tap That.

They are frustrated with the small inconveniences associated with keg ownership.

Opportunity

Potential service offerings include:

Line Cleaning Kits

Quarterly Cleaning Service

Free Line Cleaning with Delivery

Gas Bottle Exchange Program

Maintenance & Health Check Visits

These services may generate modest direct revenue but, more importantly, increase customer convenience, retention and engagement.

Overall Conclusion

While customers requested a broad range of products and services, three clear opportunities emerged:

Approachable Australian-style lagers and mid-strength beers

RTDs and alternative beverage options

Convenience-based ownership services

Taken together, these responses reinforce a consistent message throughout the census:

Customers want more reasons to use their systems and fewer hassles associated with owning them.

The strongest opportunities identified are therefore not necessarily more beer styles, but rather products and services that increase convenience, variety, participation and consumption.

The RTD finding is more commercially significant than it first appears.

The lager opportunity potentially helps you sell more systems.

The RTD opportunity potentially helps you sell more kegs to people who already own systems.

They're solving two different problems, and both are valuable. 🍻

YOUR DETAILS SECTION – post code, age, social media channel

Geography

Regional

Key Findings

49 respondents across 26 postcodes.

Strongest concentrations: 4213, 4209, 4211, 4218 and 4227.

43% of respondents came from just five Gold Coast postcodes.

Customer base is heavily concentrated within the Gold Coast.

Smaller clusters exist in Northern NSW, Brisbane and Scenic Rim.

Strategic Implications

Tap That remains a highly local business.

The core customer is a homeowner in a suburban family area.

Referral programs are likely to be particularly effective because customers are geographically dispersed throughout the Gold Coast.

Postcode distribution supports targeted delivery trials rather than a broad rollout.

Marketing should continue focusing on entertaining, home ownership and lifestyle rather than traditional craft beer messaging.

The postcode data looks exactly like where you'd expect keg system owners to live—houses with garages, patios, pools and BBQ areas. That's probably a stronger validation of the customer profile than any demographic question in the survey.

Age Profile

Combined Age Groups

Key Findings

65% of respondents are aged 41-60.

The largest single age bracket is 41-50 (39%).

Only 22% are under 40.

Customer demographics strongly align with the lifestyle and entertaining themes identified throughout the census.

Key Takeaway

Tap That's core customer is not the stereotypical young craft beer drinker.

The dominant customer is a middle-aged homeowner who values convenience, entertaining and having quality drinks on tap at home.

Strategic Implication

Future marketing, product development and service offerings should continue focusing on convenience, lifestyle and entertaining rather than highly technical craft beer messaging.

Social Media

Key Findings

Facebook: 54%

Instagram: 38%

No Social Media/Other: 8%

Key Takeaway

Facebook remains the most important social media platform for Tap That's existing keg owner base.

Strategic Implication

The survey strongly supports continuing to prioritise Facebook as the primary customer communication channel, while using Instagram to support brand awareness, visual storytelling and future customer acquisition.

Recommendation

If resources are limited, prioritise:

Facebook

Email

Instagram

for communication with existing keg system owners.

One final observation:

This result is another piece of evidence supporting a theme that emerged throughout the entire census:

Tap That's core customer is older, more established and more family-oriented than the stereotypical craft beer drinker.

That should influence not only where you market, but how you market as well.
