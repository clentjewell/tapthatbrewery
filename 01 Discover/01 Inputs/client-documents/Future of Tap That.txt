The Future for Tap That Brewery & Keghouse

1. The Problem with the Traditional Brewery Model

Most craft breweries generate revenue through three primary channels:

Taproom sales (pints)

Wholesale keg sales to venues

Packaged beer (cans or bottles)

While this model works for small operations, it suffers from a fundamental limitation: it scales linearly.

As breweries grow sales, they must also increase:

brewing capacity

staff

logistics

storage

distribution

venue relationships

Revenue growth therefore requires proportionally more operational complexity and cost.

Because of this structural limitation, most independent breweries plateau at approximately $1M–$3M in annual revenue.

Scaling beyond that point typically requires significant capital investment in production, distribution, and national retail access.

2. The Tap That Difference

Tap That operates on a fundamentally different model.

Rather than focusing only on selling beer, the company focuses on installing keg systems in homes, creating a network of recurring customers.

Every kegerator installed becomes a long-term demand node in the ecosystem.

Each installed system generates:

predictable recurring keg purchases

long-term customer lifetime value

organic word-of-mouth marketing

brand exposure within social groups

This transforms the business from a traditional brewery into a platform-style beverage ecosystem.
