## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Tap That Brewery & Keghouse is not positioning itself as a traditional craft brewery.

The company is building Australia’s leading draft beverage ecosystem, focused on creating, serving and supporting residential and commercial customers who enjoy fresh beverages on tap.

While brewing remains a core capability, the long-term value of the business is not derived from brewing volume. It is derived from the acquisition, retention and recurring consumption of active customers.

Our strategic thesis is simple:

The more Active Customers purchasing kegs from Tap That, the more valuable the business becomes.

This recurring-consumption model creates predictable revenue, strong customer lifetime value and multiple growth pathways through residential customers, venue accounts, memberships, delivery services and future expansion locations.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #1

### Tap That Has Two Economic Engines

While Active Customers remain the primary Critical Number, the business operates through two complementary economic engines.

#### Engine 1 – Residential Customers

Purpose:

Scale

Brand growth

Community

Referrals

Long-term enterprise value

Estimated Customer Lifetime Gross Profit:

$2,100–$2,400

Residential customers represent the primary pathway toward achieving the company’s BHAG.

#### Engine 2 – Venue Accounts

Purpose:

Cashflow

Volume

Market visibility

Revenue stability

Estimated Customer Lifetime Gross Profit:

~$21,840

A single venue account may generate similar economic value to approximately ten residential customers.

Venue growth should therefore be viewed as a significant strategic lever rather than a secondary business activity.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #2

### The Kegerator Is Not The Product

Traditional thinking suggests the business sells kegerators.

The planning process identified that this is not the true business model.

The kegerator is an acquisition vehicle.

Its purpose is to create recurring keg customers.

Current gross profit on a kegerator sale is approximately $300.

Estimated lifetime gross profit generated by that customer exceeds $2,400.

Approximately 87.5% of customer value comes from recurring purchases rather than the initial equipment sale.

This insight supports:

Competitive pricing on kegerators

Lease-to-buy initiatives

Referral incentives

Long-term customer acquisition investments

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #3

### Existing Keg Owner Conversions Represent The Highest-Leverage Growth Opportunity

A major discovery during the planning process was that existing keg owners may represent a more attractive short-term opportunity than creating entirely new keg owners.

Advantages include:

Existing equipment ownership

Existing keg consumption habits

Shorter sales cycle

Lower education requirements

Lower acquisition cost

Estimated lifetime value:

~$2,100

This is approximately 90% of the value of a newly acquired keg system customer.

The business should prioritise identifying and converting these customers before the market becomes saturated.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #4

### The Customer Journey Is More Important Than The Sale

The long-term value of a customer is created through habit formation rather than the initial transaction.

The desired customer journey is:

Identify Existing Keg Owner ↓ Prospect ↓ Conversation ↓ Trial ↓ First Purchase ↓ Second Purchase ↓ Active Customer ↓ Advocate ↓ Referral

The objective is not simply to sell a keg.

The objective is to create recurring customers who become advocates for the business.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #5

### Second Purchase Rate May Become One Of The Most Important KPIs

The first purchase represents trial.

The second purchase represents the beginning of habit formation.

Improving Second Purchase Rate may generate a greater long-term return than increasing top-of-funnel acquisition.

Future reporting should include:

First purchase customers

Second purchase customers

Second Purchase Rate

This metric may become one of the strongest predictors of future Active Customer growth.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #8

### The Business Faces Working Capital Constraints Rather Than Pure Profitability Constraints

The planning process identified that cash pressure primarily comes from growth-related investments rather than poor unit economics.

Primary working capital constraints:

#### Ingredients

Require upfront investment prior to revenue generation.

#### Talos Kegs

Require immediate payment and scale alongside customer growth.

These assets effectively function as a growth tax on expansion.

The planned transition toward stainless steel kegs may significantly improve long-term capital efficiency.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #9

### The Taproom Serves Multiple Strategic Purposes

The taproom should not be evaluated solely as a profit centre.

The taproom serves four functions:

#### Revenue Channel

Direct beverage sales.

#### Acquisition Channel

Approximately 20% of keg customers originated as taproom customers.

#### Brand Channel

Creates awareness and trust.

#### Community Channel

Supports events, referrals and customer loyalty.

Future strategic planning should evaluate taproom performance across all four dimensions.

## STRATEGIC INSIGHT #10

### The Real Bottleneck Is Identification, Not Conversion

The most significant growth constraint today is not the ability to convert customers.

It is the ability to identify them.

Specifically:

Existing residential keg owners are believed to represent the highest-value acquisition opportunity available to the business.

However, there is currently no scalable mechanism for identifying these customers.

The strategic challenge is therefore:

Identify Existing Keg Owners ↓ Create Opportunities ↓ Start Conversations ↓ Convert Customers

rather than:

Generate More Sales Leads

This distinction is important because it changes the focus of future marketing and growth initiatives.
