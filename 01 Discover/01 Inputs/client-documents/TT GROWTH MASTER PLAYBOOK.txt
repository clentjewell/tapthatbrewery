Part 1 — STRATEGIC FOUNDATIONS

Section 1. Vision & Strategic Philosophy

Tap That Brewery & Keghouse operates on a fundamentally different model to most breweries.

Rather than relying purely on taproom sales or packaged product distribution, Tap That is built around creating a network of keg households and venues that repeatedly purchase kegs over time.

The core idea behind the business is simple:

Kegerators create customers.
Keg refills create recurring revenue.

Every time a kegerator is installed in a home or venue, the business acquires a long-term customer relationship.

Unlike traditional retail purchases where the relationship ends after the sale, keg households continue returning to refill their kegs for months and years.

The Strategic Objective

The most important long-term objective for Tap That is:

Increase the number of keg households consuming Tap That kegs.

Everything in this playbook ultimately supports that objective.

Marketing generates awareness.
The taproom validates the product.
Kegerator sales convert curiosity into ownership.
Refill behaviour creates recurring revenue.

Over time the installed base of keg households compounds, creating a stable and scalable business.

Why This Model Is Powerful

Most breweries rely heavily on:

• taproom foot traffic
• packaged beer distribution
• retail partnerships

These models require constant effort to maintain revenue.

Tap That’s model is different.

By building a large installed base of keg households, the business creates distributed recurring demand across many locations.

Beer is consumed:

• in homes
• in venues
• in the taproom

This significantly reduces reliance on any single revenue channel.

Part 2 — CUSTOMER BEHAVIOUR

Section 1. The Four Psychological Pillars

When customers purchase a keg system, they rarely make the decision based purely on logic.

Instead, the decision is usually driven by emotional motivations, which are later justified with logical reasoning.

Through customer conversations and observation, four primary motivations consistently appear among Tap That customers:

Cost
Convenience
Connection
Kudos

These four motivations form the psychological foundation of the Tap That marketing strategy.

Every advertisement, piece of content, and conversation with potential customers should relate to one or more of these pillars.

Using this framework ensures that marketing consistently resonates with the motivations that actually drive purchase behaviour.

Pillar 1 — Cost

“Smart Drinking”

The rising cost of alcohol in Australia has become a major frustration for many consumers.

It is not uncommon to pay between $12 and $14 for a schooner at a pub.

Even purchasing cartons from the bottle shop has become increasingly expensive.

This creates a powerful opportunity for Tap That.

When customers purchase a 20L keg, the price per schooner can be as low as $2.34 per schooner, and also represents a 50% saving compared to purchasing a carton of a comparable beer.

Additionally, this further highlights a dramatic contrast with pub prices.

However, the way this message is communicated is extremely important.

The goal is not to position Tap That as cheap beer.

Instead, the messaging should emphasise smart value.

Customers are not buying lower-quality beer.
They are buying better beer at a smarter price.

Cost Messaging Examples

Advertising and content that emphasises the cost pillar may include messages such as:

“The price of beer in Australia is getting ridiculous.”

Followed by:

“$14 for a schooner at the pub.
Get beer on tap at home from as low as $2.34 per schooner.”

Another approach could highlight long-term savings:

“Your beer bill just dropped by 50%.”

The cost pillar tends to resonate most strongly with:

• High-volume drinkers
• Sharehouses
• Young families already experiencing cost of living pressures

Pillar 2 — Convenience

“The Frictionless Bar”

Convenience is another powerful benefit of owning a keg system.

A keg system removes many of the small frustrations associated with buying drinks from the bottleshop.

Examples include:

• running out of beer unexpectedly
• buying the wrong drinks for guests
• making late-night trips to the bottle shop
• dealing with large quantities of empty cans or bottles

With a keg system, drinks are always available.

This is especially powerful when entertaining guests.

Instead of worrying about what drinks to buy, the host simply pours drinks from the tap.

Another key advantage of Tap That specifically is the expanded drink range.

Unlike our competitors who focus only on beer, Tap That offers:

• beer
• seltzers
• cocktails
• spirits
• RTDs

This allows households to cater to different preferences with a single system.

Convenience Messaging Examples

Examples of convenience-driven marketing include:

“Never run out of drinks again.”

Or:

“Beer, RTD’s, cocktails and seltzers on tap.”

Content that demonstrates how easy it is to change a keg or pour drinks can also reinforce this pillar.

Convenience resonates strongly with:

• busy households
• people who entertain frequently
• workplaces hosting regular social events

Pillar 3 — Connection

“The Social Hub”

Connection is arguably the most emotionally powerful pillar in the Tap That ecosystem.

Many people are increasingly choosing to socialise at home rather than at pubs or bars.

There are several reasons for this shift:

• lower cost
• greater comfort
• family-friendly environments
• flexibility with children present

A keg system transforms a home into a natural social hub.

Instead of coordinating expensive and stressful catch ups out, friends can gather for relaxed social occasions.

Examples include:

• backyard BBQs
• watching sport
• family gatherings
• casual weekend drinks with friends and neighbours

This pillar is particularly powerful for young families and baby boomers.

Parents can socialise while children play in the backyard or pool.

Connection Messaging Examples

Examples of connection-driven marketing include:

“The best nights aren’t at the pub anymore.”

Or:

“Kids in the pool.
BBQ on the grill.
Beer on tap.”

This type of messaging emphasises lifestyle rather than product features.

Connection resonates strongly with:

• young families
• tradies hosting mates
• baby boomers hosting family gatherings

Pillar 4 — Kudos

“The Wow Factor”

Kudos refers to the novelty and excitement associated with owning a keg system.

For many people, the idea of having beer on tap at home simply feels cool.

When guests visit a house with a keg system, the reaction is often immediate.

Guests commonly say:

“That is awesome.”
“Where did you get that?”
“I want one.”

This reaction creates natural word-of-mouth marketing.

Kegerators are highly visible products that naturally attract attention during social gatherings.

This is one of the reasons the Domino Strategy (discussed later) works so effectively.

Kudos Messaging Examples

Examples of kudos-driven marketing include:

“Raising the bar on the Australian Dream. Beer on tap… in your house.”

Or:

“Everyone has a mate like this.”

Followed by footage of someone pouring a beer from their home tap.

This pillar resonates strongly with:

• younger buyers
• sharehouses
• lifestyle buyers
• people who enjoy entertaining

Why These Pillars Are Perfect For Meta Advertising

Meta’s advertising algorithm performs best when ads are built around distinct emotional angles.

Instead of running a single generic ad that simply says:

“Buy a kegerator.”

Tap That should run multiple ads built around each pillar.

Examples include:

• Cost-focused ads
• Convenience-focused ads
• Connection-focused ads
• Kudos-focused ads

Meta’s algorithm will naturally learn which audiences respond to each message.

Over time, this allows the platform to automatically identify the most responsive customer segments.

The Hidden Fifth Pillar (Optional Testing)

There is also a potential fifth motivation that may occasionally be worth testing in marketing:

Freshness

Many beer drinkers believe that beer tastes better when poured from a tap.

While this motivation is not as universally powerful as the four core pillars, it may resonate with:

• craft beer enthusiasts
• beer hobbyists
• customers who care deeply about beer quality

This message should be tested but should not replace the four primary pillars.

Key Strategic Insight

The most important insight from the four pillars is this:

Tap That is not really selling beer equipment.

Tap That is selling:

• better social experiences
• easier entertaining
• memorable gatherings
• lifestyle upgrades

When marketing focuses on these outcomes rather than the equipment itself, it becomes far more compelling.

PART 3 — THE KEGERATOR SALES ENGINE

Section 1. Kegerator Sales Using The Sell Like Crazy Framework

One of the biggest challenges in selling keg systems is that the purchase is rarely impulsive.

Unlike buying a carton of beer, purchasing a kegerator usually involves a period of consideration.

Customers often go through the following journey:

They discover that beer on tap at home exists.

They become curious and ask questions.

They imagine how the system might fit into their lifestyle.

Eventually, they decide to buy.

The problem is that this process can take months.

Many people remain stuck in the curiosity stage indefinitely. The goal is to shorten the time between discovery and purchase.

This is where the Sell Like Crazy Framework becomes important.

What Is the Sell Like Crazy Framework?

The Sell Like Crazy Framework is based on a simple principle:

People take action when they feel urgency.

Without urgency, potential customers tend to delay decisions.

The framework therefore introduces structured moments of urgency that encourage prospects to move from curiosity to purchase.

These moments include:

• competitions
• flash sales
• limited-time offers
• seasonal promotions

Instead of waiting for customers to eventually decide to buy, these campaigns create reasons to act now.

Why This Works

Most people who encounter Tap That fall into one of three categories:

1. Ready Buyers

These customers already understand keg systems and are ready to buy immediately. They have already done their research.

Their final step is to visit the brewery, ask a few questions, taste the beer, and purchase a system in the same visit.

This group represents a relatively small percentage of total prospects.

2. Interested but Delaying

These customers are interested but delay their purchase because they are:

• thinking about the cost
• discussing the decision with a partner
• considering where to place the system
• unsure about how much they would drink

Without a trigger, these customers may delay their decision indefinitely.

3. Curious Observers

These people are initially unaware, but as a consequence of exposure (eg Meta Ads, seeing one at a friends house) think the concept is interesting but have not seriously considered purchasing yet.

Marketing campaigns and product education must gradually move these people toward the interested stage.

A fourth group also exists… those that have no idea Keg Systems even exist. This represents a huge portion of the population and represents a massive opportunity.

How Sell Like Crazy Moves Customers Forward

The framework moves customers through the decision process by introducing structured marketing events.

These events create urgency and encourage customers to make a decision.

The key campaigns include:

• Kegerator Giveaway Competitions
• Flash Sales
• Seasonal Promotions
• Limited-Time Offers

Each campaign serves a slightly different purpose within the customer journey.

The Kegerator Giveaway Strategy

The Kegerator Giveaway Competition is an effective initiative to use the Sell Like Crazy methodology.

Competitions work well because they offer an attractive prize while collecting valuable customer information.

When someone enters a competition, they become a warm prospect.

They have demonstrated interest in the product and are far more likely to respond to follow-up marketing.

The Competition Funnel

The competition itself is only the first step.

The real value comes from the conversion funnel that follows.

Competition Structure

A typical competition campaign might look like this:

Prize

A complete keg system filled with beer.

The prize should be attractive enough to generate excitement.

Entry Method

Participants enter by submitting their:

• name
• email address
• phone number

This information becomes part of the Tap That marketing database.

Promotion Channels

The competition should be promoted through:

• Meta advertising
• social media posts
• email marketing
• in-taproom promotion

Paid advertising is particularly effective for reaching new audiences.

Why Competitions Work So Well

Competitions solve two major marketing problems simultaneously.

First, they generate attention and engagement.

People are far more likely to share and interact with a giveaway than a standard advertisement.

Second, they generate a database of interested prospects.

Instead of marketing to completely cold audiences, Tap That can follow up with people who have already expressed interest.

Frequency of Competitions

Competitions should not be run too frequently.

If they occur too often, they lose their sense of excitement.

Running competitions approximately twice per year helps maintain their impact.

The Strategic Value of the Competition

Even if a participant does not win the competition, they have now:

• learned about Tap That
• considered owning a keg system
• joined the marketing database

Over time, many of these participants will eventually become customers.

Flash Sales

Flash sales represent another important tool within the Sell Like Crazy framework.

These promotions create short bursts of urgency.

Examples include:

• limited-time discounts
• bundle offers
• seasonal promotions

Flash sales work best when they are:

• short
• unexpected
• clearly time-limited

Why Flash Sales Must Be Used Carefully

While flash sales can generate strong sales spikes, they must be used carefully.

If promotions occur too frequently, customers begin to wait for the next sale instead of purchasing at full price.

For this reason, flash sales should be used sparingly.

Your current approach of running them occasionally (for example around Black Friday and Father’s Day) is appropriate.

Seasonal Promotions

Seasonal campaigns can also generate excitement.

Examples include:

• summer entertaining promotions
• holiday promotions
• sporting event campaigns

These promotions tie the product to specific occasions where a keg system becomes particularly appealing.

For example:

“Get your house ready for State of Origin.”

Strategic Insight

The most important lesson from the Sell Like Crazy framework is this:

Sales rarely happen by accident.

Instead, they happen when marketing introduces timely reasons for customers to act.

By structuring campaigns throughout the year, Tap That can consistently move customers from curiosity to ownership.

Section 2. The Competition Funnel System

The Kegerator Giveaway Competition is not simply a promotional activity.

It is the entry point into a structured customer acquisition funnel.

The goal of the competition is not only to give away a keg system.

The real objective is to create a large database of warm prospects who are interested in owning a keg system.

Once people enter the competition, they enter a follow-up system designed to gradually move them toward purchasing their own system.

This section will include:

• the exact funnel structure
• the follow-up process
• the retargeting strategy
• the AIDA email sequences

Email Follow-Up Sequence

Each stage plays a specific role in the conversion process.

Stage 1 — Competition Entry

The competition begins by encouraging people to enter a giveaway for a complete keg system.

The prize should be appealing enough to generate strong interest and represent the full experience of owning a keg system.

Entry Requirements + Gleam

Participants should provide:

• name
• email address
• phone number

This information becomes part of the Tap That marketing database.

Promotion Channels

The competition should be promoted through several channels:

Meta Ads

This will typically generate the largest number of entries.

Ads should focus on the excitement of winning a keg system.

Example hooks include:

• “Win a Kegerator”
• “Beer on Tap at Home”
• “Upgrade Your BBQ Setup”

Organic Social Media

Posts should showcase:

• the keg system
• the taproom
• customers pouring beer

The goal is to make the prize feel real and exciting.

Taproom Promotion

Visitors to the taproom should also be encouraged to enter the competition.

This ensures that engaged customers also join the marketing database.

Stage 2 — Email Follow-Up

Once someone enters the competition, they should receive a structured sequence of emails.

This sequence gradually introduces the concept of owning a keg system.

The objective is to move prospects from curiosity to desire.

The email sequence should follow the AIDA framework:

Attention
Interest
Desire
Action

Stage 3 — Retargeting Ads

While the email sequence is running, Meta Ads should also retarget competition entrants.

These ads appear in the participant’s social media feeds and reinforce the marketing message.

Retargeting ads may include:

• demonstrations of the keg system
• lifestyle scenarios
• cost comparisons
• customer testimonials

Retargeting is particularly effective because it targets people who have already expressed interest.

Stage 4 — Taproom Visit

One of the most important conversion triggers is a visit to the taproom.

Seeing the systems in person and tasting the beer often removes any remaining uncertainty.

Marketing should therefore encourage competition entrants to visit the brewery.

Examples include:

• tasting events
• casual taproom invitations (free tasting paddle as a thanks for entering)
• seasonal releases

Once customers experience the product firsthand, many will begin seriously considering a purchase.

Stage 5 — Kegerator Purchase

Eventually, some competition entrants decide to purchase their own system.

This may occur:

• during the campaign (thank you page offer)
• shortly after the competition ends (via an irresistible offer) 
• months later

Even if a participant does not win the competition, the campaign has already achieved several important outcomes.

The customer has:

• discovered the brand
• joined the database
• learned about keg systems

Over time, these prospects often convert into customers.

The Long-Term Value of Competition Entrants

It is important to recognise that competition entrants represent a long-term marketing asset.

Even if they do not purchase immediately, they remain in the marketing database.

Future marketing campaigns may eventually convert them.

This is why competitions are such a powerful tool.

They generate a large pool of interested prospects that can be nurtured over time.

The real value of the competition is not the prize itself.

Section 2. The Wholesale Expansion System

(How Tap That Lands and Grows Venue Accounts)

Wholesale supply represents an important growth opportunity within the Tap That ecosystem.

4 kegs per month

48 kegs per year

This makes each successful venue account extremely valuable.

However, entering the wholesale market can be challenging because most venues already have established relationships with breweries or distributors.

Winning new venues therefore requires a strategic and relationship-focused approach.

Understanding the Venue Mindset

Venue owners and managers are constantly approached by beverage suppliers.

Most of these suppliers focus heavily on price discounts or financial incentives.

While pricing matters, it is rarely the only factor influencing decisions.

Most venue managers care about three primary things:

• reliability of supply
• quality and consistency of the product
• ease of working with the supplier

Tap That has several strong advantages in these areas.

The brewery produces its own beer, which reinforces credibility and quality.

Customers visiting the brewery can see a professional brewing operation, which builds trust in the product.

Additionally, Tap That offers a wider drink range than many competitors, including:

• beer
• seltzers
• cocktails
• RTDs

This allows venues to diversify their offerings.

The Social Warm-Up Strategy

Before approaching a venue directly, it is helpful to build familiarity.

This is done through a process called social warm-up.

The goal is to ensure that when the venue receives a message from Tap That, the brand already feels familiar.

This strategy involves interacting with the venue on social media before making contact.

Steps include:

• following the venue’s social accounts
• liking recent posts
• leaving genuine comments
• occasionally sharing their content

This should be done over several days or weeks.

By the time Tap That sends a direct message, the venue will likely recognise the brand name.

This dramatically increases the chances of receiving a response.

The Initial Outreach Message

Once some familiarity has been established, the first message should be simple and friendly.

The goal is not to immediately sell anything.

Instead, the goal is to start a conversation.

Example message:

Hi guys,

We’ve been following what you’re doing at [Venue Name] and it looks great.

We run Tap That Brewery in Burleigh and would love to shout you a tasting sometime if you’re interested.

Cheers,
The Tap That Crew

This message is casual and low pressure.

It invites conversation rather than pushing a sales pitch.

The Follow-Up Email

If the venue expresses interest, the next step is to provide more information via email.

Example email:

Subject: Tap That Brewery — Local Beer Tasting

Hi [Name],

Thanks for the reply earlier.

We run Tap That Brewery & Keghouse in Burleigh and specialise in producing fresh keg beer along with a range of seltzers, cocktails and RTDs.

Tap That Brewery

The Tasting Meeting

The tasting meeting is where the real sales conversation occurs.

The purpose of the tasting is to:

• demonstrate the quality of the beer
• explain the brewery story
• explore how the partnership could work

It is important not to overwhelm the venue with too many products.

Instead, focus on presenting two or three strong beers.

This keeps the conversation focused and manageable.

The One-Tap Entry Strategy

Many breweries make the mistake of asking venues to commit to multiple taps immediately.

This can feel risky for venue owners.

Tap That should instead adopt a one-tap entry strategy.

This means offering the venue a single tap initially.

If the product performs well, additional taps can be introduced later.

This approach significantly increases the likelihood of venues agreeing to trial the product.

• recommended retail pricing

Providing this information clearly helps venues evaluate the opportunity quickly.

Section 1. Loyalty & Membership Systems

(Strengthening the Keg Ecosystem)

Acquiring new customers is important, but long-term success depends on encouraging existing customers to continue purchasing kegs regularly.

Within the Tap That ecosystem, loyalty and membership programs play a critical role in reinforcing refill behaviour.

These programs provide several benefits:

• encourage repeat purchases
• strengthen customer relationships
• reward loyal customers
• generate valuable customer data

Tap That currently operates two key systems that support these goals:

• the Keg Crew Membership
• the Tap Tokens Loyalty Program

Together, these programs help create a strong community around the brand.

The Keg Crew Membership

The Keg Crew Membership is designed specifically for customers who own kegerator systems.

The purpose of the membership is to reward regular keg buyers while strengthening their connection to the brand.

Membership is currently priced at:

$250 per year

The program includes several benefits.

Members receive:

• $30 discount on keg refills
• 5L birthday keg for taproom consumption
• mugs at schooner prices in the taproom
• Tap That merchandise
• invitations to tasting nights
• a complimentary drink at tasting events

These benefits create a strong sense of belonging among members.

The Membership Value Proposition

The membership has been structured so that the value becomes clear to customers who refill kegs regularly.

Based on current pricing:

Customers who purchase approximately six kegs per year will effectively recover the cost of the membership through discounts alone.

Because many keg households purchase significantly more than six kegs annually, the membership becomes a very attractive offer.

This encourages customers to continue purchasing kegs from Tap That rather than exploring alternatives.

Membership Adoption

Currently, approximately 70% of kegerator owners join the membership program.

Among these members:

• roughly half join immediately when purchasing their kegerator
• the other half join later once they recognise how frequently they purchase kegs

Tap Tokens Loyalty Program

The Tap Tokens system functions as the broader loyalty program for the Tap That brand.

Unlike the Keg Crew Membership, Tap Tokens applies to all customers, including:

• taproom visitors
• keg refill customers
• merchandise buyers

Customers earn:

1 Tap Token for every $1 spent

These tokens accumulate over time and can be redeemed for rewards.

Redemption Behaviour

Customers can redeem Tap Tokens for a variety of items including:

• beers in the taproom
• merchandise
• keg refills
• other Tap That products

In practice, many customers save their tokens over time and eventually redeem them for a free keg.

The idea behind the loyalty program is to reinforce the habit of returning regularly – whether they are buying kegs or schooners

The Tap Tokens Leaderboard

An interesting feature of the Tap Tokens system is the leaderboard.

The leaderboard introduces an element of friendly competition among customers.

Some customers take pride in appearing near the top of the leaderboard.

This reinforces the kudos and community aspects of the Tap That brand.

Capturing Customer Data

One of the most valuable aspects of the Tap Tokens system is the ability to collect customer contact information.

Customers sign up using their phone number, and their payment card becomes linked to their loyalty account.

This allows Tap That to collect:

• phone numbers
• email addresses

Customers also receive 50 bonus Tap Tokens for joining the mailing list.

This provides a strong incentive to sign up and should be a priority for all staff to have all customers sign up.

Email and SMS Marketing

Once customers join the loyalty program, Tap That can communicate with them through:

• email marketing
• SMS campaigns

Email is typically the primary communication channel due to its low cost.

SMS messages can be used for targeted reminders.

For example:

• reminding customers to refill kegs before long weekends
• announcing events or promotions

Segmented SMS campaigns are particularly effective when targeting specific customer groups.

Staff Participation Challenge

One operational challenge is ensuring that staff consistently encourage customers to join the Tap Tokens program.

Improving sign-up rates can significantly increase the size of the marketing database.

Improving Loyalty Sign-Ups

Several strategies can help increase participation.

First, staff training is important.

Staff should understand that collecting customer contact information is valuable for the business.

Second, the wording used during sign-up can make a difference.

Instead of asking:

“Would you like to join our loyalty program?”

Staff can ask:

“You’ve signed up for Tap Tokens before haven’t you?”

This subtle shift assumes participation and increases compliance.

Finally, visible signage near the register can prompt customers to ask about the program.

Referral Incentives

Tap That also operates referral incentives to encourage word-of-mouth growth.

Customers receive Tap Tokens when they refer others.

Current incentives include:

• 1000 Tap Tokens for referring a new kegerator purchase
• 1000 Tap Tokens for referring a new wholesale venue
• 500 Tap Tokens for referring a new keg refill customer

These rewards motivate customers to actively promote the brand to their friends and networks.

Tasting Nights

Tasting nights are hosted quarterly and align with seasonal beer releases.

These events serve multiple purposes.

They:

• reward loyal customers
• strengthen community relationships
• introduce new beers
• create marketing content

Members of the Keg Crew program receive invitations and a complimentary drink at these events.

This reinforces the sense of belonging within the community.

Strategic Insight

The loyalty and membership systems serve a much larger purpose than simply rewarding customers.

They reinforce the central behaviour that drives the Tap That business model:

regular keg refills.

By strengthening relationships with existing customers, these programs ensure that keg households remain active members of the ecosystem for years.

PART 7 – FINANCIAL GROWTH MODEL

Section 1. The Keg Ecosystem Financial Model

(How the Tap That Business Compounds Over Time)

The Tap That business model is built around recurring keg consumption.

While the taproom generates revenue from beers sold by the glass, and wholesale venues contribute additional keg volume, the long-term financial strength of the business comes from residential keg households.

Every time a customer installs a kegerator in their home, a new keg household is created.

These households return regularly to refill their kegs.

Over time, the installed base of households grows and the total number of kegs sold increases.

This creates a powerful compounding effect.

Average Household Consumption

Based on current customer behaviour, the average Tap That household consumes approximately:

2 kegs per month

This equals:

24 kegs per year

At an average keg price of approximately:

$140 per keg

This produces annual revenue of:

$3,360 per household per year

Why Kegerator Sales Are So Important

It is tempting to view kegerator sales simply as equipment transactions.

However, within the Tap That ecosystem they should be viewed as customer acquisition events.

The true value of a kegerator sale is not the margin on the machine.

The true value is the long-term keg purchases that follow.

When a new system is installed, it creates a household that may purchase kegs for many years.

Monthly Kegerator Sales Target

A realistic growth target discussed earlier is:

15 kegerators sold per month

This equals:

180 new keg households per year

Keg Demand Created

If each household consumes two kegs per month, those 180 households eventually generate:

360 kegs per month

At $140 per keg, this represents approximately:

$50,400 per month in keg revenue

Which equals:

$604,800 per year

This revenue continues recurring each year as long as those households remain active.

Three-Year Growth Example

If Tap That consistently sells 15 kegerators per month, the installed base grows significantly over time.

Wholesale Contribution

Wholesale venues also contribute significantly to keg demand.

A typical venue may consume approximately:

4 kegs per month

If Tap That adds:

20 wholesale venues

This creates additional demand of:

80 kegs per month

Wholesale therefore complements the residential ecosystem by increasing overall keg volume.

Taproom Contribution

The taproom also consumes kegs through beers sold by the glass.

However, the taproom’s most important role is not keg consumption.

Instead, it acts as a customer acquisition environment.

Many future keg households first encounter the concept of kegerators while visiting the brewery.

The Complete Keg Ecosystem

When all parts of the business are combined, the total keg demand looks like this:

Increasing this number is the central objective of the Tap That growth strategy.

Why This Model Is Powerful

Most hospitality businesses rely on continuous foot traffic to generate revenue.

Tap That’s ecosystem distributes beer consumption across many different locations.

Beer is consumed:

• in homes
• in venues
• in the taproom

This reduces reliance on any single sales channel and creates a more resilient business model.

Strategic Focus

Because of this structure, the most important growth activities for Tap That are:

• selling kegerators
• increasing refill frequency
• adding wholesale venues

Every marketing initiative described in this playbook ultimately supports these three objectives.

Key Strategic Insight

Tap That is not simply a brewery.

It is a network of keg drinkers.

Each additional keg household strengthens the network and increases recurring demand.

Over time, this network becomes the foundation of a highly stable and scalable business.

Appendix A — Taproom Sales Conversation Guide

(Introducing Customers to Kegerators)

One of the most effective ways to generate kegerator sales is through natural conversations in the taproom.

Many customers discover the concept of beer on tap at home when they first visit the brewery.

Because the taproom displays keg systems, customers often become curious and begin asking questions.

These moments represent valuable opportunities to introduce the product.

However, it is important that staff do not approach the conversation like a traditional sales pitch.

Instead, the goal is simply to educate and spark curiosity.

The Taproom Conversation Flow

A simple conversation structure can help staff guide these interactions.

Introduce the concept

↓

Demonstrate the system

↓

Explain lifestyle benefits

↓

Answer questions

↓

Invite the customer to imagine owning one

This structure keeps the conversation relaxed and informative.

Step 1 — Introduce the Concept

Many conversations begin when a customer notices the keg systems on display.

Customers often ask questions such as:

• “How much are those?”
• “Are they for hire or sale?”
• “How long does a keg last?”

Staff can respond by briefly explaining that Tap That sells keg systems for home use.

The goal at this stage is simply to confirm that the systems are available for purchase.

Step 2 — Demonstrate the System

If the customer is interested, staff can demonstrate how the system works.

This might include:

• showing how beer is poured
• explaining how kegs are connected
• describing the gas system

Seeing the equipment in action helps remove uncertainty.

Step 3 — Explain Lifestyle Benefits

Rather than focusing purely on technical details, staff should highlight how the system fits into everyday life.

Examples might include:

• hosting backyard BBQs
• watching sport with friends
• entertaining family

These examples help customers imagine themselves using the system.

Step 4 — Answer Questions and Handle Objections

Customers often have several common concerns.

These may include:

• the upfront cost
• where the system will fit
• how much beer they might drink

• wife won’t let me

Staff should answer these questions honestly and simply.

The goal is to remove uncertainty rather than pressure the customer.

Step 5 — Invite Curiosity

Finally, staff can invite customers to continue exploring the idea.

For example:

“If you ever want to learn more about the systems, feel free to ask us anytime.”

This keeps the conversation friendly and low pressure.
